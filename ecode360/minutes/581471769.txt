Patricia Wilson, (from New Mexico) stated that there seems to be conflicting
information on the sets of drawings available for review with respect to 25 S.
Riverside Avenue; on one set the top of roof elevation is shown as 62.75 feet
and on another it is 58 feet, the height of the parapet is not defined
anywhere, and on the tallest part of the building the roof of the stair tower is
marked at 71'.9". Ms. Wilson stated that there should be another balloon
tether demonstration set at the location of the north stair tower with the
height set correctly; this would clearly demonstrate the effect this project
would have on the view corridor looking down Van Wyck towards the river.
Mayor Pugh stated that he believes that part of the confusion is the
expanding record in the sense that there are very extensive back-up
materials that include different iterations and revisions to the plans and

Village Engineer Daniel O’Connor stated that the Zoning Code allows certain
items on a roof to be exempt from the height requirement, those items
include stairway and elevator bulkheads and a parapet as well as solar

protrusions to be 20% of the roof area and the elevator and stairwell
bulkheads are way less than 20%. Village Engineer Daniel O’Connor
explained that the grading along the front of the building exposes the façade
of the building which from the street view is what you would see in terms of
the building height, on the rear of the building there is less concern because
the grade elevation goes up and less of the building will be visible when
looking down from the top of the hill.

Review of the Environmental Assessment Form Part II and the Village’s Local

project and made a recommendation back to the Village Board with respect
to the “Special Permit” and the Waterfront Advisory Committee has reviewed

adopt a “Determination of Significance” as well as a “Determination of
Consistency” with the “LWRP”. Village Attorney Whitehead explained that
the steps the Village is taking this evening is to review the recommendations
from the “Waterfront Advisory Committee” and the “LWRP” Consistency
Provisions and then make a decision as to whether the Board would like a
resolution prepared for the next meeting determining that the project is
consistent with the “LWRP”. Village Attorney Whitehead advised that the

Resolution #187 of 2020
On motion by Trustee Habib and seconded by Trustee Simon, the following
resolution was put forth for adoption:
WHEREAS on June 3, 2019, the Village Board adopted a bond resolution in the
amount of $510,000 to purchase two new dump trucks with spreaders, and
WHEREAS the Village has taken delivery of one of these trucks and anticipates the
second truck arriving in the next two weeks; and
WHEREAS the Town of Cortlandt (“Town”) has expressed an interest in purchasing
the dump trucks these vehicles are replacing; and
WHEREAS the Town has offered to purchase both trucks for $70,000 ($35,000
each), which is a fair market price and most likely exceeds what the Village would
receive at an open auction; and
WHEREAS on June 20, 2005, the Village Board adopted a resolution authorizing the
Village Manager to declare items as surplus and dispose of as necessary; and
WHEREAS per New York State GML § 103-6, municipalities are permitted to buy and/
or sell used equipment from other municipalities without offering them at auction or
public bid; and
29

WHEREAS Frank Balbi, Superintendent of Public Works, has requested that proceeds
from the sale be placed in a separate capital account for the purchase of DPW
equipment in the future,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED: that the Village Board hereby authorizes the
Village Treasurer to create such separate capital account as requested by the
Superintendent of Public Works.
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Habib stated that it looks like the due-diligence has been done and sees no
reason to oppose it.
Trustee Horowitz asked if there is a reason why these funds will only be available to
the DPW if there is a need elsewhere in the Village.
Frank Balbi, Superintendent of Public Works, stated that last year he came to the
Board asking for the replacement of two sanitation trucks; one was approved, and
the other was deferred to this year. Mr. Balbi stated that since budgeting may be a
bit tight this year, he would like to use these funds to help defer some of the costs
for new equipment. Mr. Balbi stated that the need for new equipment is not going
to go away and we are hoping to earmark those funds for future equipment
replacement.
Trustee Simon stated that this is a unique circumstance and well done on the part
of the Department of Public Works and believes putting the funds into a Capital
Account for these purposes is appropriate.
Mayor Pugh asked what the limitations would be on re-assigning those funds if it
was deemed necessary.
Village Treasurer Sandra Bullock stated that if the Board is unsure in designating
these funds, we can recognize the funds as Revenue and roll the funds into the
“Fund Balance” and at that point the Board can designate money from the “Fund
Balance” for any Capital Projects for the upcoming year.
Mr. Balbi stated that he is concerned about losing those funds and the funds being
used for something else. This is a large sum of money that could make a dent in
the purchase that we are hoping to make.
Mayor Pugh stated that the Board appreciates what Mr. Balbi has done in getting
this revenue for the Village and also his flexibility when it comes to the “Capital
30

Budget”, and in consideration of that he is inclined to support a new truck in the
“Capital Budget” but believes it may be premature to commit to the exact financing
mechanism for paying for it at this time.
Village Treasurer Sandra Bullock stated that even if the funds do not go into a
designated “Capital Account” for a DPW vehicle, the Village can always do a
transfer if we decide to use the $70,000 as a purchase for the DPW, but this must
be done before the end of the Budget Year.
Trustee Horowitz stated that while she understands that the DPW may need this, we
also have to understand that we may be in uncertain financial times and there may
be other things that we may need to consider and is inclined in keep those funds
open.

committing right now to pay for that equipment with cash and we may need to look
into bonding our “Capital Budget”.
