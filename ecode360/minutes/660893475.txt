WHEREAS, this Board has met at the time and place specified in the notice of public
hearing on the preliminary budget and heard all persons desiring to be heard

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED: that the preliminary budget as amended and
revised and as hereinafter set forth is hereby adopted and that the several amounts
stated in column titled “Adopted” in Schedule
A1 - General Fund,
F1 - Water Fund,
G1 - Sewer Fund,
HN1 – Capital Fund,
Schedule 7 - Debt Service,
be adopted and hereby are appropriated for the objects and purposes specified and
the salaries and wages stated in Schedule 5 of such budget shall be and are hereby
fixed at the amounts shown therein effective June 1, 2022.
DISCUSSION
Village Manager Healy advised that the General Fund Tax Levy of $12,655,820,
amounts to a 2.38% tax increase which is one and a third less than what we
started from in March, a house with a medium assessment of $15,800 would see
their Village tax bill increase by $102.37 which reflects a $55.00 drop from where
we were in March. Village Manager Healy explained that this number reflects a
$8,100 reduction in expenditures and an increase in a number of revenues of
$155,000, the following two changes were made in the Capital Fund: (1) due to the
Washington Engine Firehouse solar project coming in $30,000 less, “NYSERDA” has

agreed to allow the Village to use that money towards the purchase of an electric
vehicle for the Police Department which results in reducing the bond by $30,000;
(2) the “Affordable Housing Task Force” is in the final stages of publishing their
report and since we are envisioning some changes that will need to be reviewed
and studied we have put in $75,000 for Planning Advisory Services, with those two
changes the Bond Issuance is now $1,626,644. This will result in a decrease in total
debt of $312,447 in the General Fund. Village Manager Healy advised that since we
are not issuing debt in the Water and Sewer Funds the debt levels in those funds
have gone down $693,068 and $77,068 respectively, which brings a net decrease
in the Village’s total debt of $1,082,584.”
Trustee Simon stated that this was not an easy task and thanked Village Manager
Healy and Village Treasurer Tucker for all their hard work as well as the input from
our Department Heads and staff. Trustee Simon also thanked our Committees for
sharing their suggestions on our Capital programs and the public who came out and
shared their comments.
Trustee Gallelli stated that when we first received the Tentative Budget none of us
envisioned that we would end up where we are now, a lot of hard work was put into
getting us to this point and thanked Village Manager Healy, Village Treasurer
Tucker as well as our Department Heads, and staff for all their hard work.
Trustee Horowitz dittoed the comments made by Trustee Gallelli and Trustee
Simon, this first looked like a herculean-task and thanked Village Manager Healy
and Village Treasurer Tucker for their amazing work and is confident that our
residents will be happy with the results.
Mayor Pugh expressed his deep appreciation to Village Manager Healy and Village
Treasurer Tucker for their diligence, as well as the hard-work of our Department
Heads, and especially thanked the Department of Public Works who have worked so
hard to stream-line their operations in an effort to save the Village money. Mayor
Pugh expressed his appreciation to the Board for their cooperation in helping to
craft a Budget that is coming in under the “Tax Cap” despite tremendous fiscal
head-winds, but reminded everyone that this was only achievable because of the
last injection of funds from the “American Rescue Plan Act” of $400,000. Mayor
Pugh stated that this funding stream is now disappearing, and the Board will need

Resolution #59 of 2022
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ and seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York, with 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS on April 20, 2015, the Village Board of Trustees approved a resolution to
enter into an agreement to participate in a Sustainable Westchester Community
Choice Aggregation (“CCA”) program for the purpose of procuring an energy
supplier who will contract with the Village to supply energy for its residents, and
WHEREAS on October 19, 2020, the Village Board of Trustees authorized the
second iteration of the CCA program for the period beginning January 1, 2021,
through June 30, 2022, and
WHEREAS on April 4, 2022, the Village Board adopted Resolution 40-2022 to
authorize the continued participation of the Village in the CCA program, and
WHEREAS such authorization established a not-to-exceed price of 12.5 cents during
the bidding process, and
WHEREAS Sustainable Westchester is now seeking to revise the not-to-exceed price
to 13.9 cents due to the high volatility in the energy markets,

authorizes the Village Manager to execute the revised Memorandum of
