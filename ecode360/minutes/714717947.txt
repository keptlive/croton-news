WHEREAS under this section of the Village Code, the approving authority may
engage the services of a registered professional engineer to review Stormwater
Pollution Prevention Plans, specifications, and related documents, and
WHEREAS the Village Engineer is the approving authority for this work, and
WHEREAS DTS Provident Design Engineering, LLP of White Plains, New York, has
submitted a proposal to review the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan and
associated documents in the amount of $4,300,

of $4,300 with the Village to fund this work.

Village Manager Healy explained that the Village Code, Chapter 196, allows the
Village Board to retain additional professional services to review Stormwater
Pollution Prevention Plans, the Village Engineer is the approving authority in most
cases for Stormwater Permits, but because of the public concern regarding this
project, the Board has decided to have an additional review done to confirm the
analysis that has been provided by the Village Engineer. Village Manager Healy
advised that this expense will be paid for by Hudson National Golf Club.

Resolution 29 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York, with a 4-0 vote.
WHEREAS pursuant to General Municipal Law § 104-b, every municipality must
adopt a policy governing the procurement of all goods and services which are not
required to be purchased pursuant to the competitive bidding requirements of
General Municipal Law § 103, and
WHEREAS the procurement policy is reviewed annually by the Village Board and
was last adopted on December 5, 2022, and
WHEREAS New York State recently introduced the Green Purchasing Communities
(GPC) program to enable local governments to ensure that the products they are
purchasing have a lower environmental impact, and
WHEREAS any local government in New York State can participate in the program if
it has a formal purchasing policy, and
WHEREAS at a Work Session on January 30, 2023, the Board of Trustees reviewed
the requirements of the GPC Program, and
WHEREAS in order to become a Green Purchasing Community, model language
must be included in the Environmentally Preferable Purchasing section of a
municipality’s purchasing policy that reflects the local government’s commitment to
following the GreenNY specifications, and
WHEREAS the section related to exceptions to competitive bidding has also been
updated to reflect state law,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Board of Trustees hereby approves the
amended Procurement Policy for the Village of Croton-on-Hudson.
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Simon stated that in addition to the environmental benefits the Village will
also receive credits from New York State as part of the Green Purchasing Program.

Resolution 31 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton- onHudson, New York with the following Roll Call: Trustee Nicholson Aye, Trustee
Simon Aye, Trustee Horowitz Aye, Mayor Pugh Aye, motion approved with a 4-0
vote.
WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson closed on the sale of the property
located at 41-51 Maple Street in December 2022, and
WHEREAS the Village received $2,000,000 in excess of the Fiscal Year 2023 Budget
leading to an expected surplus at the end of the fiscal year, and
WHEREAS the Village has an adopted Fund Balance Policy guiding and outlining the
use of surplus funds, and
WHEREAS the Fund Balance Policy refers to the use of reserve funds as a
component of the Village’s financial planning for lawful purposes, and
17

Village Trustees Board Meeting
February 13, 2023
Page 18

WHEREAS the Village Board finds it prudent to establish a reserve fund to offset the
financial impacts of unexpected future revenue loss or expenditures,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that pursuant to Section 6-e of the General
Municipal Law, as amended, there is hereby established a contingency and tax
stabilization reserve fund in the amount of $100,000 to be known as the Tax
Stabilization Reserve Fund (hereinafter “Reserve Fund”). The purpose of this
Reserve Fund is to finance certain unanticipated revenue losses or unanticipated
expenditures chargeable to the General Fund portion of the annual budget, and to
lessen or prevent projected increases in excess of the two and one-half percent of
the amount of the real property tax levy needed to finance the eligible portion of
the annual budget, and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Village Treasurer is hereby directed to deposit
and secure the moneys of this Reserve Fund in the manner provided by Section 10
of the General Municipal Law. The Village Treasurer may invest the moneys in the
Reserve Fund in the manner provided by Section 11 of the General Municipal Law,
and consistent with the investment policy of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson. Any
interest earned or capital gains realized on the moneys so deposited or invested
shall accrue to and become part of the Reserve Fund. The Village Treasurer shall
account for the Reserve Fund in a manner which maintains the separate identity of
the Reserve Fund and shows the date and amount of each sum paid into the fund,
interest earned by the fund, capital gains or losses resulting from the sale of
investments of the fund, the amount and date of each withdrawal from the fund
and the total assets of the fund, showing cash balance and a schedule of
investments, and shall, at the end of each fiscal year, render to the Board a
detailed report of the operation and condition of the Reserve Fund. Except as
otherwise provided by law, expenditures from this Reserve Fund shall be made only
for the purpose for which the Reserve Fund is established. No expenditure shall be
made from this Reserve Fund without the approval of this governing board and
such additional actions or proceedings as may be required by Section 6-e of the
General Municipal Law and any other law; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that this resolution is subject to permissive referendum
pursuant to subdivision 2 of Section 6-e of the General Municipal Law.

Resolution 33 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton- on-Hudson,
New York, with a 4-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson utilizes handheld ticket machines for
parking enforcement, and
WHEREAS the Village is not satisfied with the level of service currently being provided,
and
WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson desires to sign an agreement with a new
provider of such devices, FBS Parking Enforcement Management Services of
Hempstead, New York, and
WHEREAS the start-up cost for the new provider is $20,000, and
WHEREAS total funding for these expenses was not included in the 2022-2023
General Fund Budget,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby
authorizes the Village Treasurer to transfer $20,000 from Contingency Account
A1990.4000 to the following account: A5650.2000
Parking/Equipment
$20,000
AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Village Manager is hereby authorized to sign
the agreement with FBS Parking Enforcement Management Services.
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Nicholson asked what the annual revenues are from parking tickets and
what is the process when a resident receives a ticket and has a question
interpreting a law.
Village Manager Healy advised that the annual revenue for parking tickets is
approximately $220,000.

before the Judge, the provision regarding the 48-hour parking ordinance has been

sort of discrepancy or question in the law, it would be reported and discussed with
the Village Attorney and if necessary, brought to the Board’s attention for further
