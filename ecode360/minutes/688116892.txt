3. PUBLIC HEARING:
A motion was made by Trustee Simon and seconded by Trustee Rosales
to open a Public Hearing to consider Local Law Introductory No. 11 of
2022 and Local Law Introductory No. 12 of 2022 to amend Chapter 230 of
the Village Code, entitled “Zoning”, determining that the Proposed Action
is a Type 1 Action under SEQRA, declaring the Village Board to be Lead
Agency under SEQRA, issuing the EAF and CAF and referring the
necessary documents to the Village Planning Board, Waterfront Advisory
Committee and Westchester County Planning Board as required by law.
Motion was approved with a 5-0 vote.

Trustee Simon stated that this is potentially a Zoning framework in which
the Board can begin the process of evaluating specific projects, he
appreciates the level of work and the theoretical scenarios under which
this has been done, and the Board will have the opportunity to make
determinations on a project-by-project basis if we go forward with this
framework.
Trustee Rosales stated that while he understands that signal timing
adjustments could improve certain scenarios, he asked how much it could
really improve.
Mr. Werner stated that those adjustments are coordinated with the New
York State Department of Transportation, we just identified these
potential measures that could be improved, but it is better to evaluate it
on a project-by-project basis.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that there are other things that can
happen in that twenty-year timeframe and that is why each project would
have to go through a specific environmental review.
Mayor Pugh asked if we were fifteen or twenty years down the line, and
the Village did not make strides in mass transit or telecommuting, and we
are stuck where we are in terms of technology, could that be a basis of
denying a project’s application.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that each project would be required to
do their own traffic studies and we would look at the impacts of each
project and if an impact is found they would need to mitigate it and find a
way to provide traffic mitigation or they might have to reduce the size of
the project to make it work.

Resolution #134 of 2022
On motion of TRUSTEE ROSALES, seconded by TRUSTEE GALLELLI, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York, with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village is seeking to replace its existing wayfinding signs, which have
been weathered over a number of years, and
WHEREAS the signs will also be updated to ensure visitors to the Village know
where municipal parking exists, and
WHEREAS the Department of Public Works has worked with the Village’s Advisory
Board for the Visual Environment on the design of the signs, and

for this purchase, and

wayfinding signs, additional funding is necessary, and

General Fund budget,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board hereby authorizes the
Village Treasurer to transfer $7,750 from Contingency Account A1990.4000 to the
following account: A3310.4200 – Traffic Control – Supplies - $7,750
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Simon stated that “way-finding” is one of the most important things a
community can do and thanked the DPW for a job well done collaborating with our
contractor, he believes that residents and visitors will be able get around town a
little bit better because of this.

WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson has owned the parcel located at 41-51
Maple Street (“the Property”) since 2008, and
WHEREAS on May 17, 2021, the Village Board of Trustees issued a special permit to
Regan Development Corporation (“the developer”) of Ardsley, New York, for the
construction of two multi-unit residential buildings at the Property, and
WHEREAS on June 7, 2021, the Village Board authorized the contract of sale for the
Property with the developer for $2,000,000, and
WHEREAS the sale of the Property was contingent upon the developer receiving
funding from the New York State Office of Homes and Community Renewal
(NYSCHR), and
WHEREAS on July 6, 2022, NYSHCR announced that the developer was awarded
funding in the amount of $3,000,000 for this project, and
WHEREAS the Village and the developer are now working towards the property
closing, and
WHEREAS the developer would like to begin site work before the official closing
takes place, and
WHEREAS an agreement allowing for such work to proceed has been drafted,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board hereby authorizes the
Village Manager to execute the license agreement with the developer subject to the
Village Attorney’s final approval.
DISCUSSION:
Village Attorney Whitehead advised that the closing will not take place until the end
of November, the only work we are going to allow them to do before they close on
the property is some tree clearing and the installation of erosion controls.
