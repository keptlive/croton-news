3. PUBLIC HEEARING #1
A Motion to open a Public Hearing regarding Local Law Introductory No. 6
of 2022 to amend Chapter 223, entitled “Water” to update procedures
regarding water service connections and meter installation and chargers
was made by Trustee Gallelli. Motion was seconded by Trustee Horowitz
and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Village Manager Healy explained that this law updates certain procedures
with respect to water service connections, the Village has also

Village Trustees Board Meeting
June 6, 2022
Page 2

transitioned from a manual reading system to an electronic system and
this law reflects this change, as well as addressing fees.
Trustee Gallelli stated that this also addresses private properties that
have Fire Hydrant connections.
Village Manager Healy explained that certain large institutions like Croton
Point Park, Skyview, Shoprite and Historic Hudson Valley are maintained
privately but the Village has to ensure that those Fire Hydrants are
inspected by a private company, these Fire Hydrants are also unmetered,
and the Village does not receive any revenue for water usage, the Village
felt that it was best to institute an annual fee to cover employee time
making sure these inspections are done and for any water that may be
used for non-fire purposes.
There being no comments to come before the Board a motion to close the
Public Hearing was made by Trustee Simon. Motion was seconded by
Trustee Horowitz and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Resolution #78 of 2022
On motion of TRUSTEE GALLELLI, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York, with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village would like to amend Chapter 223 of the Village Code, Water,
to update procedures regarding water service connections and meter installation
and charges, and
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees is considering the adoption of Local Law
Introductory No. 6 of 2022, which has been drafted for such purposes, and
WHEREAS on June 6, 2022, a Public Hearing was opened and closed, and
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED: that the Village Board of Trustees hereby
adopts Local Law Introductory No. 6 of 2022 to amend Chapter 223 of the Village
Code, Water, to update procedures regarding water service connections and meter
installation and charges, which upon adoption becomes Local Law 6 of 2022.

PUBLIC HEARING #2
A motion to open a Public Hearing on Local Law Introductory No. 7 of
2022 to Amend Chapter 86, entitled “Building Construction” to institute
the “NYStretch Energy Code” was made by Trustee Horowitz. Motion was
seconded by Trustee Rosales and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Ed Riely, 110 Truesdale Drive, Croton on Hudson stated that he is
concerned about the costs to residents, and that we do not know the
health effects from these new products.
Jonathan Katz, 34 Beekman Avenue, Croton on Hudson, member of the
Sustainability Committee, stated that the “NYStretch Energy Code” only
applies to new renovations or new construction and supports the
provisions of this law.
Lindsay Audin, 221 Cleveland Drive, Croton on Hudson, Chair of
Sustainability Committee, stated that this also applies to commercial and
municipal buildings and will help to reduce fuel and electric costs, the
changes indicated in this law are changes that have been going on in
State and Federal Codes for over thirty years. Mr. Audin added that the
solar array project at the Washington Engine Firehouse is fully financed
and will cut fuel and electric costs, this is the kind of change the Village
needs to make going forward in order to have a better future.
There being no further comments to come before the Board a motion to
close the Public Hearing was made by Trustee Horowitz. Motion was
seconded by Trustee Simon and approved with a 5-0 vote.

ENERGY AND ICE MANAGEMENT
POLICIES
Decisions on the siting and construction of
major energy facilities in the coastal area
will be based on public energy needs,
compatibility of such facilities with the
environment, and the facility's need for a
shorefront location.
Ice management practices shall not
damage significant fish and wildlife and
their habitats, increase shoreline erosion
or flooding, or interfere with the production
of hydroelectric power.
Ice management practices must consider
short and long term impacts on the Croton
River and Bay and Haverstraw Bay
significant fish and wildlife habitats.
The state coastal policy regarding the
development of energy resources is not
applicable to Croton.
