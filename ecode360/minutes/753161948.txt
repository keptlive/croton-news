1. CALL TO ORDER:
Chairman Luntz called the Planning Board meeting of May 2nd, 2023 to order at 8:02 p.m.

2. NEW BUSINESS

a) Amelia, JT, LLC (1 Niles Road 68.14-5-4/5) Referral from Village Board for recommendation
for Renewal of Special Permit for a non-conforming use in a residential zone.

Applicant John Roper and attorney Luke Hilpert were present in support of the application. The applicant
reported that there were no changes from when he originally applied for the special permit three years
ago and he submitted a letter confirming that he is meeting all the conditions outlined in the resolution for
the original special permit.

The applicant uses the space for storage of a car collection. Both Rob Luntz and John Ghegan noted
driving by the property and never seeing any activity. John Ghegan also noted that the property is always
clean when he drives by. The applicant confirmed that no one lives at the subject property.

The applicant obtained the original special permit for non-conforming use for three years and at this time
is requesting for the permit to be renewed for five years. In the spirit of compromise, the Planning Board
members recommend to the Village Board that the permit be renewed for a period of four years.

b) Porteus & Son Builders, LLC -— 25A Harrison St. (68.17-2-26.02) Minor Site Plan Application

Applicant Greg Porteus and architect Joseph Thompson were present in support of the application. Joe
Thompson stated that the proposal is for two approximately 2,600 square-foot single family homes with
basements. The homes are simple in design with attractive detailing. The applicant plans to create a
nice variation between the two homes with semi-private rear patios with some landscape screening.
They will have multiple points of egress.

Village Engineer Dan O’Connor provided comments to the architect for the retaining walls on the
property, noting that contours will need to be revised, and the elevations addressed. While discussing a
boulder wall in the rear area which is at a higher grade, the applicant stated that he could upgrade the
grading plan to make stairs or landscape boxes.


The gutter system will be connected to a cultec stormwater system and a swale area will be provided on
the side of the house to direct any runoff from the back of the yard to the street. Natural grass will be
planted. As per the NYS Stretch Energy Code 2020, the house will be made solar ready and electrical
vehicle service equipment capable.

The applicant stated that he purchased the lot behind the property, on Sunset Trail, to use for access to
the properties so as not to interfere with neighbors driving and parking and Harrison Street. There is a
road now from the lot above the property that is used to bring materials down to the construction site. A
condition will be added to the resolution that construction access from Sunset Trail be used as the main
access to the construction sites due to the narrow width of and quantity of cars parked on Harrison Street
and that upon completion of construction, the gravel construction access down to the subject property
should be restored to a grass surface or other landscaped surface.

Geoff Haynes asked about the amount of excavating that will be needed and the applicant stated that a
lot of the excavation was done by a previous owner. The applicant cleaned up the lot, took out a
dumpster load of garbage, and he did not encounter any more rock after digging test holes in several
spots. Dan O’Conner stated that if bedrock is encountered, a rock excavation plan must be submitted to
the Village Engineer.

The landscape plan shows one tree in the front yard that goes over the property line. Architect Joe
Thompson says the trunk is on the subject property, it will be a nice addition (Red Maple) and the
branches may hang over a neighboring property.

Geoff Haynes asked about the timing of construction and the applicant responded that both homes will be
put up at the same time. The applicant owns his own construction equipment which will be on site.
Construction will begin as soon as a building permit is issued and they estimate both homes will be
completed in 6-8 months. The homes will be heated with heat pumps and have electric fireplaces.

Currently, there is no plan to build a fence between the two homes. The applicant will leave the decision
regarding a fence up to the new homeowners.

John Ghegan made a motion to approve the minor site plan application, with the addition of a condition
that the construction access road from Sunset Trail to the subject property must be restored to grass or
another landscaped surface, seconded by Steve Krisky, and the motion carried, 4-0.

b) Presentation on Accessory Apartment/Accessory Dwelling Units

Jessica A. Bacher and Tiffany Zezula of the Land Use Law Center at Pace University Law School
accepted an invitation to join this planning board meeting to discuss Accessory Dwelling Units, and
participated via zoom.

Dan O’Connor started off by stating that the Village Board is considering changes to the current
accessory apartment law, and possibly adding regulations for accessory dwelling units. Tiffany Zezula
reported that many communities across the Hudson Valley are working on this issue.

Tiffany Zezula suggested that the Planning Board should begin by looking at why the Village is
considering updating the regulations, what is the purpose and intent. There was a brief discussion as to
what other local communities are doing regarding this implementing ADU regulations. Also discussed
was the need for clear definitions that would help prevent short term rentals.

If a property was put up for sale and it had and accessory apartment or accessory cottage, any potential
buyers would have to be made aware of the situation.

Jessica Bacher and Tiffany Zezula were asked if they were aware of any communities that have rejected
ADUs or made them very restrictive. Tiffany Zezula said that it is an ongoing dialogue, and new
ordinances are being drafted. ADUs are expensive, but they can add value to your home. Regarding
feasibility in different parts of town, it was noted that it will work in some locations and not in others.
Applications would have to be looked at on a case-by-case basis. Currently, Croton has a minimum lot
size, but the Westchester County model! law does not.

Additional parking is not required for ADUs. ADUs do not have to be ADA accessible.

Some takeaways from the discussion include that most communities who have ADU laws are not seeing
a big rush of applicants as it could take a long time to recap the money that it would take to build the
ADU. There many considerations regarding accessory cottages including setbacks, parking, design, and

size.

In closing, Jessica Bacher and Tiffany Zezula reiterated that Croton should look to define what this should
be for the community and create what is needed for residents.

At the end of the discussion, the Planning Board members decided to come to the next meeting with
some notes and carve out some time to draft a recommendation letter for the Village Board.

TO: Mayor Pugh and the Board of Trustees
FROM: Robert Luntz, Chairman of the Planning Board
RE: Amelia, JT, LLC Special Permit Renewal
DATE: May 17, 2023

At its regularly scheduled meeting on May 16, 2023, the Planning Board of the Village of
Croton-on-Hudson reviewed the referral from the Village Board regarding the renewal of
the special permit for Amelia, JT, LLC at 1 Niles Road.

The Planning Board reviewed the application according to the following goals as stated
in Section 230-58 of the Village Code:

A. The accessibility of all proposed structures to fire and police protection.

Amelia, JT, LLC is located on Niles Road, a village street, and therefore is accessible to
fire and police protection.

B. The compatibility of the location, size and character of the propose used with
the orderly development of the zoning district in which it is located and with
that of adjacent properties in conformity with the zoning district applicable to
such properties.

Amelia, JT, LLC has had a special permit to operate in this location for many years and
there are no changes proposed at present.

C. The safety, convenience, and congruity with the normal traffic of the
neighborhood and of the pedestrian and vehicular traffic generated by the
proposed use, taking into particular account the location and size of such use,
the nature and intensity of operations involved in or conducted in connection
therewith, its site layout and its relation to access streets.

Amelia, JT, LLC generates very little traffic in the neighborhood. At the most, there are
a couple of vans parked in the parking lot area, but the operations of the business is
very low intensity and has no impact on the neighborhood.

D. The compatibility of the location and height of buildings, the location, nature
and height of walls and fences and the nature and extend of landscaping on
the site with adjacent land and buildings and their appropriate development.

Tel: 914-271-4783 www.crotononhudson-ny.gov Fax: 914-271-3790


Amelia, JT, LLC has been operating under a special permit in this location for many
years and nothing has changed.

E. The preservation of ecological or environmental assets of the site or adjacent
lands.

This criteria is not applicable to the application as nothing has changed with the current
business.

Based on the fact that there has been no changes at this time, the Planning Board

recommends granting a renewal of the special permit to Amelia, JT, LLC at 1 Niles Road
for a period of four years.

Subdivision Plat Approval on a property which is located at 25 Harrison Street, in an RB Two-
Family Residential District, and is designated on the Tax Map of the Village as Section 68.17
Block 2 Lot 26.2.

WHEREAS, the subdivision being proposed is a two-lot subdivision; and was granted preliminary
subdivision approval by the Planning Board on May 2", 2023, and

WHEREAS, under the requirements of the State Environmental Quality Review Act (SEQRA), the
Planning Board has determined that this project is an Unlisted Action; and

WHEREAS, on June 8", 2022, the Zoning Board of Appeals approved two side yard variances
and a lot width variance from Village Code for the subdivision of a lot to build two single-family
homes, and

WHEREAS, under section 196-3(C) of the Village Code the Planning Board is the approving
authority for the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP) and such plan shall be submitted

Assessment Form (EAF) dated May 2nd, 2023 and a Coastal Assessment Form (CAF) dated

2023 for a recommendation of consistency with the policies of the Village’s Local Waterfront
Revitalization Program (LWRP) and the WAC in their memorandum to the Planning Board dated
April 26th recommended to the Planning Board that the Proposed Action be found consistent with
the LWRP; and
