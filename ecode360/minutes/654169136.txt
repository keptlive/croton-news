Trustee Simon asked the Manager to explain the “Tax Base Growth Factor.”
Village Manager Healy explained that the “Tax Base Growth Factor” is the
growth of the “Assessed Valuation” of all the properties in the Village, this
year we saw a slight increase of approximately $118,000.
Village Treasurer Tucker stated that the projected tax increase for a home
with a medium Assessment of $15,870 would result in an estimated increase
of $158.00 for the annual Village Tax.
Village Treasurer Tucker advised that the Water Fund’s appropriations of
$2,676,639 results in a proposed 5% increase bringing the water rate to
10.0343 per one hundred cubic feet of usage, there are no increases in the
Sewer Rates this year, the proposed Capital Fund expenditures includes
$625,000 for Infrastructure, $328,000 for Equipment, $700,000 for Fire
Department Equipment, $200,000 for EMS, $50,000 for the Municipal
Building, $180,720 for the Police Department, and $10,000 for the
Recreation Department for a total of $2,093,720, the proposed Capital
Project Funding includes a General Fund Debt Issuance of $1,549,644, as
well as Grant money of $284,076, CHIPS money of $250,000, and funds
from the Trust Account of $10,000. Village Treasurer Tucker noted that
between Fiscal years 2017 and 2022 the amount of Debt has decreased by
just over five million dollars (a 13.63% decrease in the total Debt) and the
total Debt for 2023 of $34,354,728 is still lower than the Debt in Fiscal Year
2022.

Trustee Simon stated that he believes that Albany may have additional
CHIPS funding and asked how this additional funding impacts the total
amount of our General Fund Debt Issuance.
Village Manager Healy stated that in addition to the $250,000 from CHIPS,
we are proposing an additional $100,000 to fund roadway improvements
through the Debt Issuance, but if we were to get the additional $100,000
from CHIPS, we could take that off, or keep it in the Budget to do more
roadway improvements.
Village Manager Healy referred everyone to his Budget Memo wherein he
explains in more detail the Bond Issuance. Village Manager Healy added that
the Debt Issuance would still result in a total reduction in the overall Village
Debt by $1,159,585. To view the Manager’s memo, you may log onto the
following:
https://www.crotononhudson-ny.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif441/f/uploads/
budget_message_2022-2023.pdf
Village Manager Healy explained that the only reason the Village was able to
appropriate such a large amount of Fund Balance last year was because of
the impending sale of the “Katz” property.
Mayor Pugh stated that this year’s appropriation of $400,000 is also backed
by the second injection of money from the “American Rescue Plan Act” which
should come in July.
Public Comment
Stuart Greenbaum, 48 Mount Airy Road, Croton on Hudson, stated that he is
surprised to see the decline in water usage considering that so many people
have been home during the Pandemic and asked if anyone knows why.
Trustee Gallelli stated that over the years the Village has made some major
water structure improvements and the MTA has also improved their water
management considerably and their efficiency has resulted in a significant
decrease in revenue.

Trustee Simon stated that residents are also conserving more by using more
efficient devices.
Village Manager Healy added that while more people were home during the
Pandemic some of our higher-end users like the schools and businesses were
closed that resulted in less water usage, additionally hot dry summers as
opposed to wetter summers also have an effect on water usage.
John Sasso, 87 Morningside Drive, Croton on Hudson, asked the Board to
consider funding a Bocce Court at Black Rock Park.
Joel Gingold, 55 Nordica Drive, Croton on Hudson, suggested that the Board
take a look at the number of Village vehicles to determine if they are all
necessary and to see if the Village can do with less, he is also concerned with
the Village’s gargantuan debt, out of the five hundred and fifty seven Villages
in New York State, Croton is one of the most indebted Villages, in contrast
the Town of Cortlandt’s Debt Service is 3% and the School District’s Debt is
8.5% of their total Budgets. Mr. Gingold stated that he understands that the
proposed Budget is only projecting about $1.5 million in new borrowing but
in the following two fiscal years the Board proposes to borrow $10.5 million
and as we know interest rates are already rising and this cannot go on, if
some unforeseen circumstances arise requiring the expenditure of perhaps
millions of dollars, we will be ill-prepared to accommodate it and is concerned
about Croton’s fiscal future. Mr. Gingold stated that he also supports a Bocci
Court.
Village Manager Healy explained that the funding sources in the Capital Plan
for the Fiscal Years 2024 and forward have not been determined, we include
all expenditures in the Capital Plan including those funded through Grants,
Trust Accounts or Debt Issuance and just because it is in the plan, it does not
mean the Board anticipates funding it exclusively through Debt.
Sam Watkins, 30 Lounsbury Road, Croton on Hudson, stated that he is not
an expert at finances, but expects that the Village Manager has done an
excellent job in looking at our shortfall and in doing the best he could to
prevent it from being a continuing problem, and supports the Budget that Mr.
