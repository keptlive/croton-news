In late July, Con Edison began offering SmartCharge Commercial (SCC), a demand
response (DR) program for EV chargers. Earlier DR discussions with a large nonutility firm found our charger load was too small to secure its attention. Under Con
Ed’s program, loads consistently reduced between 4 and 8 PM would get $10 per
reduced kW per month from June to September, and $2/kW/month in the rest of the
year. Unlike GridRewards, the starting point for measuring the demand reduction is
1

the highest load seen prior to 4 PM in each month. No advance notification is
involved. All reductions are handled remotely and automatically via for load control
software. The program also yields (for our project) a one-time ~$25K incentive to pay
for that software, regardless of the reduction in kW, though it must be >0 kW. If all 18
charge ports were used and their ~65 kW peak load dropped each month, Croton
would get ~$4,600/yr, paid in quarterly installments.
An actual power reduction may not be needed to comply. Most EVs plugging in at 8
AM should be fully charged by 4 PM. Commuters that start charging by 8 AM are thus
unlikely to notice a 4-8 PM power drop. An 8-hour charge (until 4 PM) would yield
~170 miles per workday if a charger is used by one EV or ~90 miles on a shared
charger. For details, go to https://www.coned.com/en/our-energy-future/electricvehicles/commercial-electric-vehicle-charging-station-rewards
Regarding use of Renewable Diesel (RD) in Village trucks instead of fossil-based
diesel, Mayor Pugh reported that the VB on 8/20/25 approved a pilot program wherein
3 Village diesel trucks (sewer truck, landscaper, street sweeper) will be fueled with RD
through January to assess viability. Thereafter, the VB will decide if RD will be used in
other Village diesel trucks. He said a separate fuel tank for RD will be installed at the
Municipal Place depot later this month. RD use significantly cuts both CO2e and soot.
On 8/20/25, NYSERDA began seeking proposals to build RD refineries in NY State
that would process local organic waste. A 10/6/25 webinar will provide details for
interested firms and others. In NYC, the Staten Island ferries switched to RD. They
use 400,000 gal/month, ~200X more than all Village diesel trucks combined.
On 8/12/25, Frank Balbi (DPW Supt.) informed Lindsay of a February 2025 explosion
of one of the DPW building’s two 27-year old oil-fired heating boilers. No damage or
fire resulted. The Village replaced both with propane-fired units and installed a 1,000
gallon above-ground propane tank next to the DPW parking lot. The only muni facility
still depending on fuel oil is the Washington firehouse (others use natural gas).

rooftop). $.20/W was lost on the canopy when that incentive dropped earlier this year.
Trump’s 7/7/25 executive order called for hardening IRS rules on “commencing
construction.” But the actual revisions (IRS Notice 2025-42, issued 8/15/25) impact
only projects over 1.5 MWac, requiring that they show “physical work”. Rules related
to our projects remained the same as before the Trump tax bill.
Len reported on a NY PSC five-year plan (announced 5/15/25) to spend $5 billion on
energy efficiency and weatherization programs that would include “$3.29 billion for
non-low-to-moderate-income (LMI) customers” (i.e. gross income >$108K/yr). No
federal funding is involved. Set to start 1/1/2026, it appears limited to homes built after
2010 unless previously involved in a NYSERDA or utility home energy upgrade
program. Focused on whole-home electrification, it would not fund heat pump projects
that had backup or supplementary fossil-fueled heating.

- New Business / Other Issues
Edwin reported on a proposed NY State law that would require all facilities emitting
over 25,000 (and some over 10,000) MT CO2e/yr of Scope 1 (i.e., direct) carbon
emissions to annually report them. None of the Village’s municipal buildings directly
emit more than 200 MT/yr. The largest emitter in Croton is the Metro-North train yard
at less than 7,000 MT/yr of on-site carbon, so no Croton facilities would be affected.
In late June, a NYSERDA-sponsored energy audit (free due to our CEC position) on
the Municipal Building was completed and submitted. It focused on electrification and
fuel reduction actions, but did not consider renewable options, e.g., a GSHP. Doing
the audit secured points toward our Climate Smart Community (CSC) standing.

Sustainability Committee Compost Program Expansion Proposal for 2025-2026
Proposal: The Sustainability Committee (SustComm) proposes to expand the compost program offering
by 20% while purchasing equipment to service a 40% expansion. This approach enables us to grow the
program further in coming years with an incremental increase now while staying in budget for 20252026.
Background: There are currently about 100 families signed up for the village composting program. As of
July 2025, five compost bins suffice to service those families through both low and high volume
composting days1. In 2024, there were 40 families on the waiting list for the program. Given budget
allocations for equipment in 2025-2026, SustComm can purchase two more bins. Two more bins will
enable all the families on the waitlist to participate2. Budget allocations for labor, however, allow for
only an increase of 20% more hours. Therefore, we propose to increase the program offering by 20%. A
40% increase in equipment coupled with a 20% increase in labor allows us to have the equipment for
further expansion in coming years with an incremental increase in 2025.
Conclusion: Pending committee approval of this proposal, we will advertise the expansion of the
program in the village newsletter, initiate purchase of the additional equipment, and discussion with the
Department of Public Works on the additional hours.
Frequently Asked Questions:
What will SustComm do with the budget surplus3? We reserve the surplus for unanticipated costs.
Additionally, the Committee has participated in the Croton Earth Day event sponsored by the
Conservation Advisory Council. As we have preliminary plans to expand that effort in 2026 as well, we
reserve surplus to cover that event as well.
How will SustComm advertise to recruit new participants? We propose to advertise the expansion of the
program in the village newsletter in both English and Spanish. We expect to also post flyers at the
Croton Library. Lastly, at the 2026 Croton Earth Day event, we expect to advertise the composting
program for 2026-2027.
