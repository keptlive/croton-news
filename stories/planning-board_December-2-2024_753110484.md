# Croton Eyes Cannabis Tax Revenue Sharing with Cortlandt
## Village board authorizes agreement for division of potential cannabis excise tax, despite no dispensaries currently planned.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is proactively preparing for potential cannabis sales by formalizing an agreement with the Town of Cortlandt regarding excise tax revenue. The agreement, authorized by the Village Board of Trustees on December 2, allocates 2% of the tax revenue from cannabis sales within the village to Croton, and 1% to Cortlandt. Westchester County will receive 1% as per state law.

Village Manager Bryan Healy clarified that this agreement is a preemptive measure, as "there are no current plans for a dispensary to open within the Village of Croton-on-Hudson." The resolution passed with a 4-0 vote.

**What's Next:**
*   The Village Manager is authorized to sign the Cannabis Excise Tax Agreement with the Town of Cortlandt.

# Croton Extends Kayak Concession Agreement at Echo Canoe Launch
## Hudson River Recreation will continue providing kayak rentals, lessons, and tours for another five years.

Croton-on-Hudson has extended its agreement with Hudson River Kayak Outfitters LLC, doing business as Hudson River Recreation LLC (HRR), to operate the kayak concession at the Echo Canoe Launch. # Croton Awards $73,000 Contract for Harmon Firehouse Roof
## Armor-Tite Construction of Port Chester will install a new roof as part of the EMS facility project.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is moving forward with the Harmon Firehouse addition, awarding a $73,000 contract to Armor-Tite Construction Corp. of Port Chester for roof installation. # Croton Calls on Governor Hochul to Tax Short-Term Rentals
## Village joins NYCOM in urging state to level playing field for hotels and address housing shortage.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is urging Governor Kathy Hochul to sign legislation that would subject short-term rentals to sales and occupancy taxes, similar to hotels and motels. The resolution, passed by the Board of Trustees on December 2, highlights that current state law exempts short-term rentals from these taxes.

Trustee Simon, who brought the resolution forward, noted that this issue is a high priority for the New York Conference of Mayors (NYCOM). He stated that collecting sales tax "is a very worthwhile effort to be pursued at the State level." The board voted 4-0 in favor of the resolution.

# Croton Covers Lifeguard Overtime with Budget Adjustment
## Interfund transfer addresses unexpected personnel costs at Silver Lake.
