Tony Granger, 217 Half Moon Bay, Croton on Hudson, stated that he
understands the Village is getting a fee of $1,120 for use of the train station
parking lot for the music festival, but is the Village also getting a cut on the
entrance fees, the application that was submitted last year for the event was
not Franki Leon but rather a Peekskill Entertainment Agency, last year the
music was very loud, and believes that 1000 people is an understatement.
Village Manager Healy stated that the applicants will also be responsible for
overtime costs which the Village approximates to be $15,000 which must be
paid in full before the event. Village Manager Healy explained that the
reason that an admission charge is allowed is that the train station is not a
park and does not fall under those regulations and we allow admission fees
for other events at the train station.
Susan Skrelja, 56 Truesdale Drive, Croton on Hudson, asked the Village to
slow down all of the building and housing initiatives until we know what the
impacts will be from our current developments. Ms. Skrelja stated that she
recently learned that Croton is a “Pro Housing Community” and according to
the “Home and Community Renewal” website, of the eleven mid-Hudson
communities under that program, only two are villages, one being Croton on
Hudson, the others are cities and we as a village of just over 8,000 residents
should not feel that we must be the saviors for affordable housing.
Lauren Davis, 14 Hamilton Avenue, Croton on Hudson, stated that she
opposes six story buildings in Croton and does not want to see zoning
changed to accommodate these types of buildings.
Alex Skrelja, 56 Truesdale Drive, Croton on Hudson, stated that he is not
against development, but asked that we slow down to see how things work
out before we move forward with more development. Mr. Skrelja shared an
email communication where Mayor Pugh where he states: “that the 41-51
development would likely be the last of this kind for the foreseeable future
of Croton, and that the thirty-three units of affordable housing are an
important downpayment on closing our deficit for affordable housing and
any new affordable housing in the coming decade will be smaller
developments”. Mr. Skrelja and asked what percentage of units would be
affordable for the proposed LOT A development.
Mayor Pugh stated that under the AHOP Program, (Affordable Home
Ownership Opportunity Program) there is the potential for the LOT A
development to be 100% affordable, but the exact composition has not been

that we were looking at a 20% affordable rental option, the developer is also
seeking the opportunity for affordable ownership which is distinguishable

stated that this would be a chance for people to build generational wealth,

Naureen Shabid, 1608 Half Moon Bay, Croton on Hudson, stated that both

including all necessary documents and exhibits, and to undertake any actions necessary

Resolution #146 of 2024
On motion of TRUSTEE MURTAUGH, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton- on- Hudson, New
York with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees has received a special permit application from
Sprocket Power of Bedford Hills, New York, proposing the installation of an integrated
energy system at Croton Auto Park, located at 1 Municipal Place, comprising of a battery
energy storage system with a 280 kWh output, and
WHEREAS a Site Development Plan has been submitted with the application, and
WHEREAS Tier 2 Battery Energy Storage Systems are permitted through the issuance of
a special permit by the Village Board of Trustees within the C- 2 zoning district, and
WHEREAS this action is a Type II action under the New York State Environmental Quality
Review Act, which does not require an environmental review,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby refers the
special permit application to the Planning Board for a recommendation back to the Village
Board of Trustees, and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Village Board hereby directs the Village Clerk to
notify the Westchester County Planning Department of this application in accordance with
law.

Village Board of Trustees
July 17, 2024
Page 8

DISCUSSION:
Trustee Murtaugh stated that the main reason this has come into play is that the parent
company of Dodge/Chrysler/Jeep/Stellantis has insisted upon electrification capabilities at
all of their dealerships nationwide and they are taking advantage of Croton’s embrace of
modern technology.
Village Manager Healy explained that when the Board amended the Solar Law, we
removed the requirement for a Special Permit and it now just requires a Building Permit,
the only aspect of their plan that the Board is required to review is the battery storage
system.
Trustee Simon stated that it is a commitment on the part of Croton Auto Park to make a
long-term investment in Croton since it involves a significant amount of dollars to do this
project.
Mayor Pugh asked if this information has been shared with the Fire Department.
Village Manager Healy advised that he did share this with the Fire Department, they have
provided their comments with the Village Engineer and those comments have been given
to Sprocket Power and incorporated into the agreement.

Resolution #150 of 2024
On motion of TRUSTEE SIMON, seconded by TRUSTEE MURTAUGH, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton- on- Hudson, New
York with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson has owned a parcel located at Croton Point
Avenue, also known as Tax Map Parcel 79.17-1-5 (“Lot A”) since the 1960s, and
WHEREAS Lot A was utilized as overflow parking for the Croton-Harmon Train Station for
many years, and

Village Board of Trustees
July 17, 2024
Page 15

WHEREAS since the COVID-19 pandemic reduced the demand for commuter parking, the
Village Board of Trustees has determined Lot A is not needed for municipal purposes and
issued an RFP for the development of Lot A, and
WHEREAS on April 24, 2024, the Village Board adopted Resolution 86-2024 to move
forward with WBP Development LLC (“the Applicant”) of Chappaqua, New York, as the

Trustee Simon explained the history of how Croton became a Pro Housing
Community, two years ago Governor Hochul developed a Legislative package
that required mandatory metrics for municipalities to meet in terms of
housing and if a municipality did not grant what a developer wanted, then
the developer had the right to go to the State and the State could take over
the Zoning of that particular municipality, that proposal was defeated in
Albany, but the message that was sent to the Governor, that if the State
wants more housing, the way to do it, is with incentives and out of that
discussion the Pro Housing Communities Program was created. Trustee
Simon stated that this program simply recognizes communities that are
committed to incremental growth over an extended period of time, and under
the leadership of Village Manager Healy we submitted the proper forms and
received the Pro Housing Community designation. Trustee Simon stated that
this designation gets us in the HOV lane for some helpful grant programs
that deal with infrastructure programs that benefit the entire community.
