4. PUBLIC HEARINGS
Public Hearing #1
A Motion to open a Public Hearing to consider issuance of a Special Permit
to convert the existing mixed-use building at 193-195 Grand Street, a
nonconforming use, to an all-residential building with five one-bedroom
apartments, another nonconforming use was made by Trustee Horowitz.
Motion was seconded by Trustee Simon and approved with a 5-0 vote.

Village Trustees Board Meeting

Luke Hilpert, Attorney representing the applicant stated that his client is
requesting a Special Permit to convert an existing mixed-use building
located at 193-195 Grand Street to an all-residential building with five,
one-bedroom apartments. Mr. Hilpert stated that based on the Planning
Board’s suggestions, they have made some changes to the plans.
Trustee Simon advised that he was at the Planning Board meeting when
this was discussed and believes that the applicant has done a good job
and has responded to what the Planning Board was looking for.
Ron Napolitani stated that he believes the architect has come up with a
good solution to the Planning Board’s concerns.
Trustee Gallelli stated that the proposal is consistent with other buildings
in the immediate area and likes it very much.
Trustee Nicholson referred to some pictures associated with a former
tenant of the property and while she understands there are going to be
checks and balances during the approval process, she would like to go
over exactly what tenant’s rights are and how we make sure tenants are
protected.
Village Manager Healy explained that any complaint with regard to
housing conditions would go to the Engineering Department and the Code
Enforcement Officer would investigate, if there were violations that were
not remedied, they would be prosecuted in the Village Court.
Mr. Hilpert stated that the tenant in question had ample opportunity over
the years to file a complaint, but did not do so, over the past four years
there have been two separate families in that same apartment and no
complaints were brought forward. Mr. Hilpert stated that a lot of the
pictures were taken when the tenant occupied that unit more than four
years ago and some of what is alleged in that letter is outdated or a result
of the tenant’s use. Mr. Hilpert stated that Mr. Napolitani is committed to
addressing the issues but stressed that these are allegations and is
concerned with adding a statement in the resolution and asked that the
word “addressed” be changed to “considered” since some of them are
unfounded.

Trustee Nicholson stated that it is important that we are clear about what
the applicant is going to do and what the Engineer is going to inspect and

Resolution 1-2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees has received a special permit application
from the property owner of 193-195 Grand Street to convert the existing mixed-use
building, a nonconforming use, to an all-residential building with five one-bedroom
apartments, another nonconforming use, and
WHEREAS a change from a nonconforming use to another nonconforming use is
permitted under section 230-53A (2) of the Village Zoning Code if deemed to be the
same or of a more restricted nature by the Village Board of Trustees, and
WHEREAS this action is a Type II action under the New York State Environmental
Quality Review Act, which does not require an environmental review, and

WHEREAS on December 5, 2022, the Village Board referred the special permit
application to the Village Planning Board and Westchester County Planning Board as
required by law, and
WHEREAS this action is a Type II action under the New York State Environmental
Quality Review Act, which does not require an environmental review, and
WHEREAS on December 5, 2022, the Village Board referred the special permit
application to the Village Planning Board and Westchester County Planning Board as
required by law, and
WHEREAS the Westchester County Planning Board has responded that the
application is a matter for local determination, and
WHEREAS the Village Planning Board has provided a memorandum of support for
the special permit application, and
WHEREAS a public hearing was opened and closed on Monday, January 9, 2023,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby issues
a special permit to the property owner of 193-195 Grand Street to convert the
existing mixed-use building, a nonconforming use, to an all-residential building with
five one-bedroom apartments, another nonconforming use, with the following
conditions:
1. That the applicant will work with the Planning Board during site plan review
to make improvements to the current rendering to make the building façade
and patio areas architecturally more exciting and vibrant for the
neighborhood.
2. That the existing deficiencies be addressed during project construction.
3. That the applicant considers the installation of solar panels on the roof of the
building.

Public Hearing #2
A Motion to open a Public Hearing to consider the renewal of a Special
Permit for the operation of a day care center at 10 Old Post Road South
was made by Trustee Horowitz. Motion was seconded by Trustee
Nicholson and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Desiree Drapala, owner of Happy Hearts, stated that her family has operated the
day care center at 10 Old Post Road South since 2010, they currently have one
hundred and fifty children with forty employees.
Village Manager Healy advised that as far as he knows there have been no issues or
complaints against the day care center.
Trustee Nicholson stated that after reviewing the application she noticed that the
owner’s city and zip code were not included on the application and asked that it be
revised to include that information.
Village Manager Healy stated that he will speak with the Village Engineer about
updating the form so that there is a section to include this information.
Debbie Braddick, 9 Young Avenue, Croton on Hudson, stated that the facility runs
very well, and it is good for the community, but does not understand why this
Special Permit couldn’t be granted without them having to go through this process.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that this is a use that requires a Special Use
Permit, the Village Board has traditionally required renewals of these permits, but
more recently the Board has been renewing them without an expiration. Village
Attorney Whitehead advised that this permit is being renewed without an expiration
and there will be no need for them to apply again.
