       2. NEW BUSINESS
    a) DiCairano, Maria - 4 Kuney Street (68.17-3-37) Application for an Accesso,y Apartment

Maria DiCairano was present in support of the application. Ms. DiCairano did a large renovation over a
year ago and the handicapped accessible apartment was part of the project. Ms. DiCairano was under
the impression that the apartment was already approved, but she applied over a Zoom call during Covid
restrictions and it seems that there was some confusion. During the final inspection of the project, the
inspector pointed out that the stove in the basement made it an accessory apartment and that brought to
everyone's attention that the apartment needed to be legalized. Ms. DiCairano stated that the space
meets the requirements for an accessory apartment and complies with the square footage regulations.
The apartment is in one garage bay and part of the basement. Rob Luntz looked at the drawings
submitted by the architect and believes that they are accurate. Eva Thaddeus asked about parking and
the applicant says that there are spots for three cars. Rob Luntz went over the rules of the accessary
apartment stating that if the property changes ownership, it is the current homeowner's responsibility to
notify the buyer to reapply if they would like to keep the accessory apartment. Neighbor Cecilia Albano
from 5 Kuney Street spoke and noted that the block is one-family houses and she would like it to stay that
way. Rob Luntz reiterated that this is still a one-family house with an accessory apartment, it cannot be
sold as a two-family house. Dan O'Connor reminded all that an accessory apartment must be owner
occupied.

Eva Thaddeus made a motion to approve accessory apartment application with the conditions specified in
the resolution, seconded by Steve Krisky, and the motion carried, 5-0.

    b) Ella 1, LLC, 193-195 Grand Street (68.17-4-58) Site Plan approval for three new one-
       bedroom apartments
Owner Ron Napolitini and Engineer Gregory Caccioppoli, P.E. were present in support of the application.
Gregory Caccioppoli stated that he did the site plans and structural design for the three units that are to
be added to the structure. The existing condition drawing shows current setbacks, erosion control
measures, structures to be removed and synopsis of the coverages on the lot that were used to calculate
storm water mitigation. What they are proposing will be designed for a 25-year storm. Gregory
Caccioppoli says that they will be removing the existing porch in the rear of the building and rebuilding a

porch that will wrap around the building and maintain access to the second floor. In the new plan, there
will be access to the basement from the exterior of the building and pleasant modifications to aesthetics in
the front of the building.

The applicant stated that the last time he was before the Planning Board, he heard what they said about
making improvements to the front of the building, and he hopes that the board is happy with the revised
proposal. Rob Luntz replied that he did notice the improvements and thinks they did a good job. Geoff
Haynes asked and it was confirmed that there are two two-bedroom apartments upstairs and this
application is for three new one-bedroom apartments downstairs. Rob Luntz asked and it was confirmed
that in this district, you need one parking spot for each dwelling unit, so including the building next door
which has four units, they are required to have nine parking spots. The applicant's proposal has ten
parking spots behind the building, and there are five on-street parking spots on Grand Street, in front of
the building.

Rob Luntz asked about the fire hydrant and if it was accounted for in the plans. Greg Caccioppoli says
that yes, the fire hydrant is shown on the utility plan. The applicant says that as long as you can get a
spanner wrench and a hose on the hydrant, it meets requirements. The applicant and/or Village Engineer
will speak to DPW and the fire department and if they can move it a foot or so, they will. If the fire hydrant
cannot be moved, the applicant will make it work.

Steve Krisky asked about plans for solar. The applicant stated that he is speaking with a local solar
company regarding another building that he owns. The applicant is undecided if he will put solar on this
building as it will depend on the pricing and if it makes sense financially. Rob Luntz suggests getting the
building at least solar ready as it is cost effective to do this during renovation.

Geoff Haynes asked about the utility plans and how the applicant plans to meter individual tenants for gas
and water. The upstairs apartments in this building will be gas fired for hot water. For the three one-
bedroom apartments that are proposed, there will be mini splits and heat pumps. There will not be
individual gas meters for each unit. There will be one water meter from the village that the applicant will
sub-meter for each unit. There will be individual gas meters for the two upstairs apartments and
downstairs there will be electric that can be individually metered. Geoff Haynes also asked if any issues
with the sewer connection will be addressed and the applicant stated that there were no sewer problems
to his knowledge, other than an apartment with a clogged drain. At no point since he purchased the
building did he have to have the sewer cleaned out in the street.

The Planning Board discussed with the applicant where the utilities would come down from the second
floor to the first floor. Gregory Caccioppoli stated that they could box something out in the corner of each
unit or if there is not enough room, they could build a 12" by 12" chaise. It was noted that a four-inch
waste line typically fits in a wall and that this should not be an issue.

Once again, the issue was raised that these proposed one-bedroom apartments have two full bathrooms.
The applicant replied that it is a nice apartment and the second bathroom could be for a guest. Village
Engineer Dan O'Connor was asked if anything would change if this was used as a two-bedroom
apartment and Dan O'Connor said that in this zoning district, the parking rules require only one parking
space per unit, so that would not be an issue. If there was a second bedroom, a smoke detector would
have to be installed.

John Ghegan asked about electric vehicle chargers. Electric vehicle chargers were not part of the plans,
but it was noted that the applicant could possibly run a conduit to have a charger as now would be the

     c) Murphy, Andrew- 118 Scenic Drive West (67.10-2-5.2) Minor Site Plan Application for New
        Single-Family House
Keith Staudohar of Cronin Engineering and Justin Kacur, Architect of Highlands Architecture were
present in support of the application. Keith Staudohar reports that this project is for the last of a recently
approved three lot subdivision. The site is currently vacant. The proposal is for a single-family residence.
The proposed residence and driveway are well within the confines of the originally approved limits of
disturbance. A steep slope permit is not required. They are connecting to existing services that are
stubbed into and near the property corner with an easement on the adjoining property. Keith Staudohar
reported speaking with Dan O'Connor and getting some minor technical comments which will easily be
addressed.

Justin Kacur passed around some material samples (roofing, siding, trim) to be used in the project and
Rob Luntz asked for a quick synopsis on the history of the subdivision for newer members of the Planning
Board. Justin Kacur stated that there was originally an office building on the proposed site, that was
purchased by their clients who worked with Cronin Engineering to get the zoning changed, and
subdivided the property into three lots. Their clients reside at the property built on one lot, then they
designed House A which was built and sold and this is House B. After preliminary review of the original
drawings for this house, the applicant rotated the house and moved the garage to the back of the
property. Overall, it is a single-family residence with approximately 2,200 square feet of habitable space
with a two-car garage complete with electric vehicle charging. The windows will be clad wood (on House
A they used aluminum clad wood by Anderson Windows and will probably use something similar on this
home). The siding will be fiber cement by James Hardy. They punched out a small gable in the front of
the house and another small gable on the back to add space and character. The applicants believe that
the proposed residence fits in with the character of the neighborhood. There is a full basement. When
asked about the lot width, the applicant replied that they received a zoning variance for the width of the lot
and Dan O'Connor noted that that was one of his comments that the plans should include a note that a
variance was granted.

When asked about tying into the sewer district, Keith Staudohar replied that they are in the district, so it
should not be an issue beyond applying for a permit and paying a fee.

The applicants are not calling for any trees to be removed, but any trees that are leaning or dying or dead
will be taken down. There will be erosion control, a row of silt fence.

John Ghegan made a motion to approve the Minor Site Plan application for a new single-family house,
seconded by Geoff Haynes, and the motion carried, 5-0.

        b)   Hudson National Golf Club Wetlands Activity Permit Application

 The Planning Board Members received three letters from Village residents regarding a Wetland's Activity
 Permit Application for Hudson National Golf Club. Geoff Haynes asked about the scope of the project
and Dan O'Connor stated that HNGC plan to replace their entire irrigation system, redo the bunkers and
 redo the greens and a small part of that work is in wetland buffer zones, some of which are constructed
wetlands, built when HNGC was developed in the mid-1990s. The Waterfront Control Commission had
one meeting regarding this application, but they did not issue a determination as they asked for additional
information. The Village Attorney is also researching SEQR regulations. Additionally, another application
was in front of the Village Board for community solar on Prickly Pear Hill Road and the Village received
two letters (one from Matrix, the solar company leasing the land from HNGC and one from HNGC)
withdrawing the application. The solar application was officially withdrawn. An audience member called
out that there was an additional third applicant who filed a permit for solar at HNCG and Dan O'Connor
stated that he will discuss the possibility of a third application with the village attorney and if there is a
third application, that entity would also need to withdraw the application. Geoff Haynes asked about the
connection between the solar application and the wetland's permit and Dan O'Connor explained that
there is wording in the wetland law that says if there is another pending application on any other part of a
property, then the Wetland Permit application must go to the Planning Board. The other application is
now withdrawn, so the wetland permit should stay with the WCC as they would be the approving
authority. Steve Krisky said that if it was sent to the wee by mistake, then the way to undo the mistake is
to send the wetland permit to the Planning Board. Dan O'Connor said that the village attorney would
have to answer to that.

Geoff Haynes asks if the WCC does approve the wetland's permit, what is the Planning Board's role in
giving oversight on this project as that there is a possibility for run-off and potentially some things that
need close review. Rob Luntz said that the village attorney should answer as to whether the Planning
Board should take a look at this application. Steve Krisky mentioned three "pretty damning" pieces of
correspondence sent to the Planning Board suggesting strong issues raised about the SEQR
determination. Steve Krisky also mentioned a letter from Brett Scales of HNGC detailing the project and
some discrepancies regarding the amount of soil/disturbance that needs to be stored. Steve Krisky also
brought up the Short Environmental Assessment Form (EAF) signed by Ralph Mastromonaco, HNGC's
engineer, and questioned Mr. Mastromonaco's documenting that the total area to be disturbed is 9.6
acres of the golf course. Steve Krisky went on to say that due to the inconsistencies in the letter from
HNGC and the EAF, and because the numbers are so close (9.6 acres of land disturbed, just under the
SEQR cut off of 10 acres), why not err on the side of caution and do a Type I application which triggers
much greater environmental review as well as Planning Board review. Eva Thaddeus said that Steve
Krisky made a strong case. Geoff Haynes said that any project should get a review where village
residents have some level of comfort that it was reviewed and meets regulations and an engineering
review to changes in topography. Rob Luntz agreed and stated that this project is potentially larger than
