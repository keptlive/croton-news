4. PUBLIC HEARINGS:
a. Motion to open a Public Hearing on Local Law Introductory No. 3 of
2022 to amend the Code of the Village of Croton on Hudson to reflect
the abolishment of the position of Village Justice effective January 1,
2023, and the consolidation of The Village Justice Court with the Town
of Cortlandt was made by Trustee Gallelli. Motion was seconded by
Trustee Simon and approved with a 5-0 vote.

Village Manager Healy explained that the consolidation of the Village
Justice Court can only be made at the conclusion of our Village
Justice’s term which ends this year and since the Village Justice term
is four years, we are only able to consider this once every four years.
Village Manager Healy stated that comments have been made that the
Board should not factor the parking lot revenue into this because some
feel that it will come back. Village Manager Healy explained that since
the pandemic began, the parking lot remains about fifty percent of
what it was pre-pandemic and based on what we have seen, we
believe that a number of commuters have permanently switched to
working from home or a hybrid schedule and as a result they are not
renewing their permits. Village Manager Healy stated that the Court
does not cover its costs and over the past six fiscal years (FY16
through FY21), the court has run an average deficit of $97,750, if we
consolidate, the Village will continue to maintain a portion of revenue
from Village cases adjudicated in the Town Court, and under State Law
the Village is entitled to fines issued under Village Ordinances which
include parking tickets, violations of the Zoning Code and dog
violations, the Village is also entitled to the local surcharge of any
handicapped tickets issued in the Village.
To view a detailed analysis, you may access Village Manager Healy’s
“Justice Court Consolidation Proposal” by logging onto the following
link:
https://www.crotononhudson-ny.gov/sites/g/files/vyhlif441/f/
uploads/court_presentation.pdf
Village Attorney Whitehead explained that under New York State
Village Law, the abolishment of a Village Justice is subject to a
“Permissive Referendum”, thirty days after the Board acts, a petition
can be filed requiring it to be put to a “Referendum”, the petition must
be signed by at least twenty percent of those who were registered to
vote in the most recent Village election (1,061), and upon receiving
the petition, the Board would then schedule a special election. Village
Attorney Whitehead added that the Board could of its own act, at least
2

fifteen days prior to the date of the election, repeal the law and
eliminate the need to go ahead with the special election. Village
Attorney Whitehead also explained that the Village Board at any point
in time could re-establish the Village Justice position through a Local
Law which would be subject to a “Permissive Referendum” as well.

Mayor Pugh thanked everyone for coming out this evening and
speaking on this matter, everyone has given the Board a lot to think
about, but at the end of the day, there is an important economic
consideration here and would be best thought of in a larger context as
part of the overall Budget and believes we should defer making a
decision on this matter until the Manager has had a chance to present
the preliminary Budget.
Mayor Pugh stated that if we take Justice Watkins estimate of
$158,000 of savings to the Village, it is more than the combined cost
of all programs for Seniors and Youth, and while he is not saying that
we make a tradeoff, this is to give everyone the sense of the
challenges we are facing. Mayor Pugh stated that along with high
inflation, the consumer price index growing at approximately seven
percent annually, a property “Tax Cap” of two percent, as well as our
“Non-Property Tax Revenues” continuing to remain fairly depressed, it
is going to be intensely challenging to find savings in this Budget.
Mayor Pugh stated that while people may say that you cannot make
judgements based on forecasts about variable revenues, this is what
the Board has to do every Budget season and we have to make our
best good faith estimates on those variable revenue sources. Mayor
Pugh reminded everyone that in 2021 we thought that our revenue
sources would come back when the vaccines came out, and then we
were hit again by the new Delta and Omicron wave and revenues have

narrowly escaped persecution and possibly death in their home country following
the Taliban’s takeover of the United States –aligned government in Kabul last
summer, have arrived in the United States, sometimes with only the clothes on
their backs and the hope in their hearts, and
WHEREA, they have left behind family members, friends and neighbors, careers and
a familiar culture and a way of life, and
WHEREAS our new neighbors must make the difficult physical, cultural, emotional
and career transition to a new home, a new life and a new country, and
WHEREAS Ossining for Refugees, the newest Community Sponsorship organization
in Westchester County, bringing together neighbors, houses of worship, community
organizations and key service providers in Ossining, Briarcliff Manor, Pleasantville

and Croton-on-Hudson, has been formed to help resettle the many Afghan
individuals and families who deserve a new start, and
WHEREAS Ossining for Refugees works with HIAS, (the Hebrew Immigration Aid
Society), the oldest of nine federally recognized resettlement agencies, Hearts &
Homes, the leader among several Community Sponsorship Organizations in
Westchester County, and Catholic Charities, and
WHEREAS Ossining for Refugees is made up of all volunteer-staffed teams to
provide assistance to refugees and asylees in the following areas: (1) securing safe,
comfortable and affordable housing, (2) providing furniture, including beds, new
mattresses, bedding and towels, housewares, dishes, kitchen/household supplies
and personal care items, (3) introducing families to community amenities, (4)
evaluating language needs and providing appropriate support, (5) identifying
educational needs of family members and interfacing with school districts for
appropriate placements and advocacy, (6) identifying potential employment
opportunities, (7) helping to understand finances and create workable household
budgets, (8) providing transportation to doctor’s appointments, school meetings,
etc., (9) helping to apply for Social Security cards, SNAP benefits, ID cards/Driver’s
Licenses and other government programs, and (10) helping families navigate the
medical insurance system, and

Refugees and other nonprofits in welcoming and re-settling our new Afghan
