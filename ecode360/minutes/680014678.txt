3. PUBLIC HEARING
A motion was made by Trustee Gallelli to open a Public Hearing to
consider the issuance of a “Special Permit” for a twenty-nine-unit multifamily development located at 1380 Albany Post Road. Motion was
seconded by Trustee Simon and approved with a 3-0 Vote.
Kory Salomone, from the Law Firm of Zarin and Steinmetz stated that
they believe the project is fully compliant with the new “Gateway District
Regulations” that were adopted in Local Law #3 of 2022, the plan calls
for the demolition of the existing building, replacing it with a 33,400

(eighteen one-bedroom apartments, eleven two bedroom apartments,

charging stations, barbeque area, and bocce court. Mr. Salomone stated
that as outlined in his letter, they believe that this project complies with

WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees has received a special permit application
from the Matra Group for a 29-unit multi-family development at 1380 Albany Post
Road, and
WHEREAS the project is located within the 0-1 Limited Office and North End
Gateway Overlay Zoning Districts of the Village, where multi-family developments
are allowed by special permit, and
WHEREAS the Board of Trustees issued a negative declaration under SEQRA and a
statement of consistency with the Village’s LWRP in connection to this project on
March 7, 2022, and
WHEREAS the Board of Trustees referred the application to the Planning Board on
July 5, 2022, and
WHEREAS the Planning Board reviewed the request and has submitted a memo in
support of the application, and
WHEREAS ten percent of the dwelling units will be affordable as required by Section
230-48 of Chapter 230 of the Village Code which will result in three units, two (2)
one bedroom and one (1) two-bedroom apartments; and
WHEREAS on August 15, 2022, a public hearing on granting a special use permit to
the Matra Group was opened and closed,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby
issues a special use permit to the Matra Group for a 29-unit multi-family
development at 1380 Albany Post Road subject to the following conditions:
1. That an application for site plan approval and subdivision approval
(consolidation) be submitted to the Planning Board.
2. That project amenities be included and detailed further in site and
architectural plans.
3. That a reasonable number of charging stations for electric vehicles and
bicycles be incorporated into the project.
4. That the applicant work with the Planning board and NYSDOT to provide
reasonable pedestrian facilities which may include use of the bike and
walking trail on the West Wind HOA property in addition to other facilities.

8. PUBLIC COMMENTS ON NON-AGENDA ITEMS
Rolf Lamar, 228 Hessian Hills Road, Croton on Hudson, stated that he
opposes the proposed Homeland cell tower, he believes that any claim that
9

Village Trustees Board Meeting
August 15, 2022
Page 10

the tower will improve emergency communications for the community is
misleading, it is his understanding that no one has done a basic analysis to
ascertain if there will be any benefit to the Fire Department, Police
Department or the DPW Department, Fire and Police Department
communication frequencies are different than wireless carriers and to
improve these communications the very first step is to test the entire
coverage area to identity the existence, locations as well as geographic
boundaries that may have gaps and only if this information is determined
then you can begin the process to figure out the best location to install a
tower. Mr. Lamar stated that it does not make sense to build a new tower
and move over antennas in the hopes that it will improve emergency services
communications because no one has made the effort to figure out the UHF
and VHF coverage status and by not determining these factors it will be
detrimental to the Village because we will end up with more towers than we
actually need.
Signe Bergstrom, 228 Hessian Hills Road, Croton on Hudson, stated that this
proposal appears to be based on an anecdotal assessment, as far as she can
tell no comprehensive assessment has been done to determine gaps in our
Village, when asked at a meeting to see that data, the Board was told that
they did not have that information on hand, when the assessment was done
why wasn’t the findings not present in the proposal and why have they not
been shared with the public, without a real assessment of the scope of the
problem, Croton residents will be subject to a hodgepodge leaving an
irresponsible approach to selecting sites, the construction of a tower of this
size will require a crane on a large flat bed truck, the roads to this site are
extremely narrow, lined with trees making it very difficult to get to, trees will
most likely have to be taken down on both Glengary and Hessian Hills Roads,
she knows that the Village owns this land and can break any of its Zoning
Codes but the chances of a private resident building such a tower on the
property line of their neighbors would be very low, Homeland specifically
targets municipalities that have not enacted codes governing communication
towers and while the FCC grants municipalities the right to enact their own
codes, Croton which has sparse codes must operate using the FCC codes
which limit municipal government controls, the installation of the Homeland
