- Reports
Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS):
Bernie is attending a 5-part series of webinars on BESS, and reported on items in the first 2. He
reviewed differences between the two main types of lithium-ion batteries: LFP (lithium iron
phosphate) and NMC (nickel manganese cobalt), and among residential, commercial, and
utility-scale BESS. He displayed a map showing major installations (of which there are many)
working or in process across the U.S. He focused on issues related to fire safety and facts-vsfiction thereof. Many improvements in fire codes and technology have occurred since the April
2019 fire and explosion at an Arizona utility battery. After some discussion regarding how this
may impact the proposed 2.7 MW (13.5 MWh) battery proposed for our train station, Lindsay
agreed to query Len (a member of Croton’s fire brigade) as to the training our firefighters have
received (or should get) regarding BESS.
Bio-based fuels for trucks and heating fuel:
Niall and Lindsay reviewed the types of heating and diesel fuels used by Con Ed and the
Village. The Village uses B5 versions (i.e., containing 5% bio-based fuel) for both from Sprague
Energy. Niall reported that Con Ed’s trucks use B20 from spring to fall, and B10 during the
winter, due to issues with B20 at low temperatures. He pointed out that steel fuel tanks
(regardless of end use) that were painted on the inside would need to be replaced with
aluminum tanks. Otherwise, B10 or B20 might cause the paint to peel off, clogging fuel filters.
For the Village, requests for quotes for B10 heating fuel found that Sprague provided only B5.
Robison sells B10 for about $.25/gal more, i.e., ~$2,300 a year. For truck diesel, Sprague’s
upcharge for B10 is about $.16/gal (B20 was ~$.76/gal more). Switching to B10 diesel could
then cost ~$5,000 a year. Both actions together would cost ~$7,300/yr and cut our carbon
footprint by ~19 MT/yr (about 1.3%) at a price of ~$380/MT. Carbon offsets cost <$10/MT. We
await NYSERDA’s answer on using grant money to pay for extra fuel costs or carbon offsets.
A similar review of the school district’s heating and diesel bills found no use of bio-based fuels.

--------Appendix: review of prior discussion on how to spend grant money. Note some pricing has been
updated since this appeared in February’s minutes (e.g., quotes on bio-based fuels).
Regarding how best to spend the grants (if we get them), see below in approximate order of
cost-effectiveness for cutting GHG. All are evaluated based on a ~15-year lifetime. NYSERDA
CEC funding is unlikely for the first three [but we continue to await answers on those options].
We agreed that they could be considered without using grant money.

4

1. Buy carbon offsets to zero out the municipal carbon footprint at a price of ~$5/MT. Our
present footprint is ~1,400 MT/yr, so annual cost would be ~$7K/yr. The 15-year cost would be
$105K.
2. Buy down 100% renewable NYPA power by its extra ~5 mills/kWh (1 mill =$.001) for RECs.
At .7 lb/kWh, we get a price of ~$16/MT. Annual cost would be ~$8K/yr yielding a 15-year cost
of $120K. [Note: REC prices have been rising since February 2021].
3. Buy down B10 for diesel trucks and fuel oil at the DPW based on its extra $.10/gal. At 22.2
lb/gal diesel and a ~10% carbon cut, GHG is saved at ~$100/MT. Annual cost would be
~$3.5K/yr leading to a 15-year cost of $52.5K. [Recent quotes are about double that amount,
and the GHG savings should be relative to existing B5, not standard diesel].
4. Convert the Washington Engine firehouse from oil heat to air-source heat pumps (ASHP).
Based on Jon’s draft design, the capital cost is ~$30K. Replacing its ~2,500 gal/yr of fuel oil
with ~27,000 kWh/yr to run heat pumps, would cut ~16.6 MT GHG at ~$125/MT. If 15-year
operating savings of ~$2K/yr are included, that cost drops to about zero.
5. replace resistance DHW heaters at the DPW and Muni Bldg with HPWH at net .5 MT/yr per
site, and per site cost of ~$2,000, yielding GHG savings at ~$270/MT. Because of NYPA
demand charges, operating cost savings may be small. Installation and GHG pricing to replace
the Washington firehouse’s oil-fired DHW system would be higher because it instead uses oil
and also incurs demand charges. The other two firehouses use natural gas. While their DHW
replacements would net ~.14 MT/yr per site, operating costs would increase.
6. To replace residential resistance units with HPWH at Con Ed rates, a ~2/3 reduction in kWh,
and a net cost (after rebate and tax credit) of $700, GHG cost is under ~$100/MT. If operating
savings are included, that would be less than zero. Without rebate and tax credits, it is about
zero.
7. New EV sedans at ~$30,000 (after $5K/car State subsidy), at net 3 MT/yr per car, GHG
savings would cost ~$700/MT. If operating savings are ~$.03/mile, 15-year savings would bring
that cost down to ~$650/MT. Two cars are old enough to merit replacement: Rec. Dept. 2001
Taurus 19 MPG (veh. #94-10) and the Eng. Dept. 2002 Crown Victoria 18 MPG (#102-02). Both
are parked at the Muni Bldg, and would use its two (presently public) charge ports. [A 3rd EV
was purchased in late May 2021 at a cost of ~$33K to replace a parking enforcement car.]
By comparison, rooftop PV (@ $2/W, .7 lb/kWh) cuts GHG at a cost of ~$300/MT. For
canopies, that cost may be 50+% higher. However, community solar leasing rent avoids a need
for grants. Brian asked that Lindsay refresh Ecogy’s earlier offer for rooftop PV at several muni
sites. [Due to reductions in VDER credits since February, community solar projects smaller than
~200 kWp are not financially viable, but may be pursued as solar leases with a grant.]
Pending acceptability from the Village manager and Board, the committee approved the above.
