Village Engineer Daniel O'Connor introduced the project. Angelo Sgobbo and Matt Hunt, representing
Westchester County, joined the meeting via Zoom to present an overview from the previous meeting for a
Wetland Activity Permit application for the full replacement of the existing Quaker Bridge over the
Croton River. The applicants noted the project was currently out to bid, with the bid submissions due
shortly. The County expected to evaluate responses and award the contract soon, with construction
anticipated to begin in October or November 2025. The replacement would include all new abutments and
substructure, with the new two-lane steel truss bridge expected to open to two-way traffic by the end of
June 2026.

1

Mr. Hunt addressed potential environmental impacts. He stated that the new bridge would have a slightly
larger footprint, but impacts to the floodplain had been thoroughly documented in the permit application.
It was stated that the water surface elevation was expected to remain unchanged, and the new bridge
would be constructed on piles in the same general location as the current abutments. The foundation
design would provide greater resilience to future storm events. Scour protection measures, including
heavy stone armoring and erosion control on steep slopes (particularly in the southwest quadrant), would
help protect the structure and surrounding environment.
Chairman Greenbaum thanked the applicants for the overview and turned the meeting over to the Board
for questions. Mr. Wiegman noted that the project was well documented and that outstanding questions
had been addressed in previous meetings. The Board agreed and no further questions were raised.
Daniel O'Connor provided an update on regulatory coordination. Permit applications to both the New
York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) and the U.S. Anny Corps of Engineers
remain under review. Mr. Hunt noted that while responses had not yet been received, the Anny Corps
typically has 45 days to complete its review. A Memorandum of Agreement was reported to be
established between the County, the State Historic Preservation Office, and the Village to document and
mitigate the removal of the historic bridge structure.
Additionally, the applicants added that a temporary construction easement had been submitted for a
section of the southeast quadrant of the bridge site, which is Village-owned property. It was also noted
that the County issued a SEQRA Negative Declaration, and the Village Planning Board found the project
consistent with the Local Waterfront Revitalization Program (LWRP), thereby concluding the
environmental review process.
Chairperson Greenbaum read the prepared draft resolution into the record and noted that no public
comments had been submitted. All application materials were made available on the Village's website.
Following review, the Commission approved the wetland permit with conditions, including compliance
with approved plans and permits, notification to the Village Engineer prior to commencement of work,
on-site posting of the permit, erosion and sediment control measures, submission of a Stormwater
Pollution Prevention Plan (SWPPP), and inspection requirements. It was noted that the permit would
expire on December 31, 2026, or upon project completion, whichever occurs first.
MOTION: Mr. Wiegman made a motion to approve the wetland activity permit with conditions for the
complete replacement of the existing Quaker Bridge over the Croton River. Seconded by Mr. Pfrang.
The motion was carried unanimously with a 4-0 vote. Roll call votes were as follows: Mr. Wiegman yes, Mr. Pfrang -yes, Chairman Greenbaum-yes, and Dr. Shanks-yes (Mr. Wetherbee, absent).

(Sec. 67.19 Block 5 Lot 1 & Se. 78.8 Blk. 1 Lot 1), Senasqua Park (Sec. 78.12 Block 1 Lot 1),

PRESENT: Thomas Lewis, via Zoom.

Mr. Lewis, agent for Trillium Invasive Species Management, appeared via Zoom to request the renewal
of a multi-year Wetlands Activity Permit. The permit covered targeted herbicide treatment of invasive
plant species, including Phragmites australis (common reed), Japanese knotweed, and Porcelain berry
across several Village-owned parks and properties. The locations included in the application were: Croton
Landing Park, Senasqua Park, Duck Pond Park, Kaplan's Pond Open Space Area, and Gouveia Park.
Village Engineer Daniel O'Connor introduced Mr. Lewis, noting that Trillium had been providing
invasive species management services to the Village for the past seven years. Mr. Lewis provided a
comprehensive update on the program's progress and challenges. He explained that while infestations had
been substantially reduced in most areas, some regrowth continued, especially in "difficult-to-access" or
rocky riprap zones, such as those at Senasqua Park. He noted that deep root systems had made complete
eradication challenging.
Mr. Lewis reviewed updated environmental and permitting requirements. Mr. Lewis stated that due to
changes in New York State Department of Environmental Conservation (DEC) regulations, certain
locations now would require additional state permits before herbicide application can continue. However,
in order to obtain a DEC permit, a local wetlands permit from the Village is needed first. Gouveia Park,
for example, did not fall under DEC's jurisdiction since it is adjacent to a waterpark, not a wetland, and
therefore only requires Village approval.
Mr. Lewis reported that Croton Landing was the only site currently subject to new DEC permitting, as the
area is officially classified as a wetland. Kaplan's Pond was previously reported to be free of Phragmites,
but recent monitoring revealed isolated patches of regrowth, and Trillium intended to apply for a DEC
permit for that site as well. Duck Pond, Senasqua, and Gouveia Parks are not expected to be impacted by
new DEC rules.
Mr. Lewis walked the Board through each park, using documentation and photos included in the
application materials. He also clarified that the full report covered all of Trillium's work for the Village,
not just work covered under the wetland permit. For example, Black Rock Park was discussed, but was
not part of the application since it lied outside the wetland buffer zone.
At Croton Landing Park, Mr. Lewis noted that the majority of Phragmites has been successfully
eliminated. Commissioner Leo Wiegman remarked on the substantial improvement compared to photos
from 15 years ago, calling the site a "success story" from the Board's perspective. John Bouchard from
the Department of Public Works mentioned a new infestation in the park, and Trillium would submit a
permit request to address it preemptively.
At Gouveia Park, Japanese knotweed remains the primary concern, particularly in the downhill area near
the parking lot. Areas adjacent to the house had already been cleared. In Kaplan's Pond Open Space Area,
regrowth of knotweed was identified along the stream overflow areas, particularly in the lower part of the
park. Senasqua Park continued to experience regrowth in rocky areas and along riprap, although it had
previously shown no signs of infestation for a period of time.

3

Mr. Lewis also discussed additional species, including Tree ofHeaven (Ailanthus altissima), which has
started to spread in some parks. In response to a question from Dr. Shanks, Mr. Lewis explained
Trillium's herbicide injection technique, which involved inserting a needle with a side hole directly into
the plant stem. This targeted method minimizes environmental impact and is only used on stems at least
¾ inch in diameter.
Regarding public notifications, Mr. Lewis clarified that yellow pesticide application signs are posted at all
treatment sites, even when not required by regulation. The signs would remain posted for at least 24 hours
and are removed once the product has dried and the area is safe. The only location where neighbor
notification had been required in the past was at Kaplan's Pond Open Space Area. Mr. Lewis confirmed
that Trillium did not and would not exceed the application amounts that would trigger mandatory
neighborhood notice.
The Commission commended Trillium for its thorough reporting, responsible application methods, and
overall success in managing invasive species across the Village's parks. Mr. Wiegman and Chairperson
Greenbaum praised the visible progress, especially at challenging sites like Croton Landing.
Chairman Greenbaum read the draft resolution for review.
No public comment was received.
