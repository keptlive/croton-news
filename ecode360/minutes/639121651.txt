Village Manager Healy explained that the change is for housekeeping
purposes only, the Village’s “Steep Slopes” law originally adopted in 1989
and revised in 2008 through Local Law #3 changed the approval authority
from the Village Board to the Planning Board and at that time the “Trees”
chapter was not amended to reflect this change and we are seeking
approval to make that change this evening.
John Ealer, 5 Prickly Pear Hill Road, Croton on Hudson, stated that
because of the importance of trees and steep slopes we encourage and
empower public comment periods throughout the process at both the
Village Board and Planning Board levels.
Mayor Pugh emphasized that there is no change in the public’s input
process established in the Code.
Earl DePass, 10 Nordica Drive, Croton on Hudson, stated that because we
had a developer who took down more trees than what was approved, this
is not enough and until the Village is able to beef up its monitoring the
Village should not be making any changes unless there will be more
personnel that will go out and monitor these sites

we also have an Assistant Village Engineer who serves as our Inspector as
well as our Code Enforcement Officer.

deviated wildly from the original approved plans, fines were issued and
collected for the replacement of trees that were wrongfully removed and
the property owners were required to re-apply for their “Site Plan”
because they had gone so far from what they had originally proposed to
the Planning Board.

1.5% of the taxes generated, if you look at the one million dollars in

study found that home prices within .36 miles around a new
dispensary fall by an average of three to four percent and urged the
Board not to allow this.
Earl DePass, 6 Nordica Drive, Croton on Hudson, asked that the Board
put the decision off until we have a public forum on whether Croton
should allow dispensaries, last week twenty-three municipalities in
Westchester opted out, most of them held public meetings, there is an
epidemic of drug addiction in this Village and urged the Board to hold a
public forum.

Regular meeting, and during those meetings there was not a majority
of people that were in favor of opting out, procedurally it would be
difficult for us to do so at this point since opting out requires the
adoption of a Local Law, and we do not have any meetings scheduled
until after the first of the year. Mayor Pugh stated that our primary
concerns have been about public consumption and people driving
under the influence which is why we opted out of cafes and lounges,

marijuana is legal for adult use, and we will need to remain robust in
our education about the harms of marijuana and other drugs for
people with developing minds and bodies.

previous meeting is that the licenses are going to be forthcoming for
vendors to apply in 2022/2023, the possibility of someone in Croton
getting a license is an uphill struggle because of the competition that
