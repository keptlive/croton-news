Responses to questions and Concerns:


most of the properties along Wells Avenue vary in elevation from
eighty feet to the mid-seventies, the height of the building is
twenty-eight feet and someone standing in one of the backyards on
Wells Avenue would look over the top of most of the structure and
their view would not be blocked.



in addition to receiving approvals from the Croton Planning Board,
the developer will need to secure approvals from other government
agencies, and they are anticipating that the project could start
around April of next year with eight to ten months to fully complete.



the dumpster to the nearest property is approximately sixty-five
feet and between the elevation difference and the fifty-foot
vegetative buffer homeowners should not be affected



contact information can be provided to everyone if they need to



a “Traffic Report” was conducted and was included in the
submission, a final detailed report will be issued to the “Department
of Transportation” as part of the process; the “DOT” will review all
of the traffic movements and all of the proposed mitigations for the
project; since 129/Maple Street is a State Roadway, the developer
will need a permit to attach utilities, stormwater and install
sidewalks



the “Traffic Study” was conducted during peak-hour trips, two way
volume on “Maple Avenue” was approximately seven hundred
vehicles per hour, two-way volume on “Municipal Place” was
approximately six-hundred and on “Riverside Avenue” the peak
hour volume ranged between two and four hundred per hour



the current proposal is to install a sidewalk on the west side of
Maple and run it south from the proposed entrance of the project to
“Municipal Place”; the developer is also looking at having a crosswalk installed at that location since there is a handicap curb-cut on
the opposite side of the street (to view diagram, refer to “Concept
Plan”, Figure 2), they are also proposing to maintain the mid-block
crossing that is next to the bus stop but that will be determined by
the “Department of Transportation”



with respect to the school bus question, next to the driveway
entrance there is a sidewalk with a handicap ramp, and they
envision the school bus going to that location so that students can
use the internal sidewalk to get on an off the bus



Village Attorney Whitehead responded to the question if this is a
“done deal” and explained that the process includes land use
approvals as well as the Board’s approval of the sale of the
property, in terms of the land use process, the developer will need
a “Special Use Permit” which is what this public hearing relates to,
“SEQRA Determination” under the “State Environmental Review

Rose Noonan, Executive Director, “Housing Action Council” stated
that they market many of the affordable housing developments in
Westchester County and follow “Westchester Affirmative Marketing
Requirements” and well as “New York State’s Affirmative Marketing
Requirements”, and it is their responsibility for determining
eligibility; it is a very broad based marketing approach through
advertising and working very closely with community groups, they
conduct workshops to make people aware that a project is being
developed in their community; they plan to work very closely with
Croton and the local community groups to get the word out so that
Croton residents, workers and volunteers are aware of this
opportunity.

Mayor Pugh stated that the pedestrian issue is a valid concern, and we will do
everything that we can to make this area pedestrian friendly, but it is
important to keep in mind that the existing sidewalk now stops at Olcott and
children on Wells Avenue do not currently have the benefit of a sidewalk; it is
not a good thing but is the reality of the situation and something that we
need to work on.
Mayor Pugh stated while the Board would like to have this project begin soon
but there are still a lot of things that need to be resolved; some of which are
with the Planning Board and others with the “Department of Transportation”
as well as other agencies that have jurisdiction.

Mayor Pugh stated that the “RFP” indicated that the Village would not
consider anything with less than a twenty percent affordable housing
component and Regan Development met this requirement as well as coming
in with the highest price as well as not including a commercial aspect into
their proposal which the Board felt better suited this area.
Mayor Pugh stated that the addition of the three bedroom apartments is to
ensure that this project scores as well as possible when it is evaluated by the
“New York State Department of Housing and Community Renewal” for its
application for “Low Income Housing Tax Credits”; with respect to the
“PILOT”, the final contract is still being negotiated, presumably the three
bedroom apartments will increase the revenue from this project, and we are
looking to have that revenue shared equitably.

environment where all people are accepted and respected by virtue of their intrinsic
humanity, and where all people can contribute their unique and special gifts for the
