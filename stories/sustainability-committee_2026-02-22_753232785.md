# Croton-on-Hudson Goes Green: Village Fleet Now Runs on Renewable Diesel
## Croton's 18 diesel vehicles are now using renewable diesel, cutting the village's carbon footprint by 12.5% without additional cost to taxpayers.

Croton-on-Hudson has switched its entire fleet of 18 diesel vehicles to renewable diesel, marking a significant step in the village's sustainability efforts. The move, announced on January 23, 2026, will reduce the village's carbon footprint by 12.5%, eliminating 186 metric tons of CO2 emissions annually.

"All of the Village’s diesel-powered heavy-duty vehicles (e.g., garbage trucks and snow plows) now run on Renewable Diesel (RD)," according to a press release. Unlike traditional biodiesel, renewable diesel is chemically identical to petroleum diesel and made from fats and oils like soybean or canola oil.

Sustainability Committee Chair Lindsay Audin spearheaded the initiative, calling it the "Village's largest single measure to reduce greenhouse gas emissions to date." Superintendent of Public Works Frank Balbi oversaw the technical implementation, ensuring the fuel could be used as a "drop-in" replacement. The cost of the renewable diesel will be covered by the village's sustainability fund, which is supported by revenue from the community solar array on top of the Department of Public Works (DPW) building.

# Croton Considers Moving Salt Shed to Boost Battery Storage Project
## Relocating the salt shed at Municipal Place could free up space for a larger battery energy storage system (BESS), potentially increasing revenue for the village.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is exploring the possibility of relocating its salt shed to the Westchester County salt storage site at Croton Point Park. This move would free up the current salt shed site at Municipal Place for expansion of the proposed Battery Energy Storage System (BESS) project.

According to the February 22, 2026 Sustainability Committee minutes, relocating the salt shed would allow the village to store seasonal yard waste at the Municipal Place site, "free[ing] up space to maximize potential BESS MW (and rent)." Village Trustee Len Simon and Mayor Brian Pugh discussed the potential move, noting it is feasible but "isn’t likely until later this year."

Moving the salt shed would also address concerns about water contamination at the current site, according to Mayor Pugh. Sustainability Committee Coordinator Lindsay Audin is drafting a Letter of Intent to "lock up" 5 MW of hosting capacity for the BESS project while discussions continue. The committee is also exploring a mini-RFP and other mechanisms to comply with state regulations for municipal public works projects.

# Croton Food Scrap Recycling Program Expanding to All Residents
## Croton-on-Hudson is opening its food scrap recycling program to all residents, with new collection bins delivered to the site on February 19.

Croton-on-Hudson is expanding its food scrap recycling program to include all interested village residents. Two new large green collection bins, known as "toters," have been delivered to the designated site as of February 19, 2026.

Cheryl Wong, a member of the Sustainability Committee, is preparing announcements for village newsletters, local media, and an email blast to current participants. The announcements will inform residents about the expanded program and how to participate.

The Sustainability Committee thanked Frank Balbi and the DPW "for handling the purchase and delivery of the toters."
