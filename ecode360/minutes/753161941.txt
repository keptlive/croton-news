Planning Board Meeting Minutes

1. CALL TO ORDER:
Chairman Luntz called the Planning Board meeting of April 18th, 2023 to order at 8:01 p.m.

2. NEW BUSINESS

   a) ARQ.Architecture P.C. - 119 Scenic Drive West (67.10-2-4) Minor Site Plan

Jorge Hernandez, architect, was present in support of the application. The homeowners have requested an expansion of the lot's building envelope to build a larger rear deck to replace the existing rear deck. The proposed new deck extends approximately two feet outside the building envelope and under the conditions of the approval for this subdivision, any construction outside of the building envelope needs to come before the planning board. The proposed improvement complies with the zoning code.

John Ghegan made a motion to approve the minor site plan application, seconded by Steve Krisky, and the motion carried, 5 -0.

   b) Metro Physical Therapy - 46 Maple St. (79.9-1-77) Change of Use Application from food service to physical therapy space

Architect Benjamin McCarthy was present in support of the application. Metro Physical Therapy will be taking over the vacant tenant space formerly occupied by Capriccios Pizza on Maple Street. The applicant will have to come in to get a building permit to build out the floor plan. With this type of business, less parking spaces are required. The applicant stated that there will be six staff members and at peak times, there would be 6-8 people receiving therapy, with each session being approximately one hour. Chairman Luntz states that it will be good for that space to be occupied. John Ghegan mentions that there are two handicapped spots outside the space and wonders if they will need additional handicapped spots. Steve Krisky wonders if the planning board could issue a condition to have an electric vehicle charging station. It was decided that the Metro Physical Therapy employees should be asked to park in the parking area to the east of the building, leaving the parking spots in front for the clients.

Geoff Haynes made a motion to approve the change of use application for Maple 2005 LLC, located at 38-48 Maple St. with a condition that employees of the facility park in the spaces to the east of the building, seconded by Steve Krisky, and the motion carried 5 to 0.

   c) Porteus & Son Builders, LLC - 25 Harrison St. (68.17-2-26.02) Preliminary Subdivision

Greg Porteus of Porteus & Son Builders was present in support of this application. Greg Porteus showed drawings of the two homes that he would like to build at this location. A previous owner filed a subdivision application for this property in 2014 and planned to build a duplex, similar to a neighboring property. The applicant wants to build two single family homes, so he went to the zoning board and received variances on June 8, 2022, granting side width variances and lot width variances. According to the applicant, during the public hearing for the zoning board, the neighbors expressed their preference for the proposal of two one-family houses.

When asked about construction traffic on Harrison Street, the applicant reported that he purchased an unbuildable lot on Sunset Trail, behind the proposed subdivision site, to eliminate trucks and other construction vehicles on Harrison Street. Once the project is complete, he will seed the Sunset Trail lot, and possibly sell it to a neighbor. The excavation of the site is 95% complete.

Steve Krisky made a motion to call for a public hearing for the application of Porteus and Son Builders at 25 Harrison Street and to declare the Planning Board lead agency and to send the referral to the Westchester County Planning Board and to the Waterfront Advisory Committee for review, seconded by Eva Thaddeus, and the motion carried 5-0.

Geoff Haynes asked about the height of the proposed homes and how it compares to the adjoining duplex and the applicant says that the heights are similar.

It was noted that on the EAF, the box was checked Yes for the question "Is the project site, or any portion of it, located in or adjacent to an area designated as sensitive for archaeological sites on the NY State Historic Preservation Office (SHPO) archaeological site inventory?" so the applicant will have to reach out to SHPO to notify them of his plans.

3. OLD BUSINESS

   a) Boulos, John - 30 Piney Point Avenue (79.13-4-44) Amendment of minor site plan approval for single-family house construction to modify certain aspects of the land, architectural and site plans.

John Boulos of 30 Piney Point Ave. was present in support of the application. At the request of the applicant, he Planning Board members went by the property to see a portion of the carport that was wrapped in wood. This improvement was proposed instead of the stone veneer that was on the original planning board resolution. Rob Luntz thought it was a vast improvement, and suggested maybe some type of metal to cover the gap at the top of the wood in order to avoid a maintenance problem. Steve Krisky asked the applicant if he planned to stain the wood and the applicant said that he was not and that it was the same color as the deck and that the color would fade in. After hearing the applicant's explanation, the planning board agreed.

The applicant also provided a letter from his landscape architect with an update on the plantings to date.

John Ghegan made a motion to approve the amended minor site plan application, seconded by Eva Thaddeus, and the motion carried, 5 to 0.

   b) Regan Development Corp. - 41-51 Maple St. (aka "Katz Property" 78.12-3-3) Amended Site Plan and Amended Tree Removal Permit

Keith Libolt and Peter Schuyler were present in support of the application. Dan O'Connor reported that the original application was approved by the Village Board and Planning Board, but now as construction has started, there are some field changes. Keith Libolt describes working with the neighbors to bring a pleasant resolution to issues that have arisen during construction. Some neighbors have requested additional work scope, particularly a neighbor whose house is next to where a sidewalk will be added is requesting additional grading and a retaining wall to be built on the edge of his property, as shown on the revised plans.

The applicant says that there are discrepancies between the existing tree plan and the landscape plan about which trees stay, so the applicant would like everyone in agreement as to what trees come down. Additionally, a test slope was cut for the neighbors to see what the hill will look like and the neighbors requested a fence for safety reasons.

Additionally, the applicant's structural engineer stated that two retaining walls on the plans could not be built the way they were proposed so they will be eliminated from the project and the money originally proposed for these two walls will be offered to the neighbors for additional screening and landscaping, in a neighborly consideration. The applicant also plans to clear out the northeast corner of the site that is full of garbage, regrade it and plant trees.

Geoff Haynes asked about the DOT's involvement with the sidewalk construction and the applicant replied that he had a permit from DOT. There is one tree that was supposed to remain that is partially in the DOT right of way and partially on the subject property, but it must come out because of the elevation change and the need to make the area level.

Steve Krisky asked about documenting the changes that are on neighboring properties and Dan O'Connor replied that the applicant will need to mark up the tree removal sheet with any changes and that the Planning Board needs specific information regarding grading and plantings on the Katz Property. There are at least three additional tree removals that are not marked on the plans and Dan O'Connor stated that now is the time to get all tree removals on record. Keith Libolt said that he would like to walk the site with Dan O'Connor to make sure that they get it right.

The applicant says that he will apply for the permits for the work that needs to be done on the neighbor's property and record it all on an "as built." Attorney Peter Schuyler is representing all the homeowners on the street and reports that he has had multiple meetings with the applicant, trying to find solutions for all. Peter Schuyler stated that he can draft a term sheet to memorialize the agreement. Geoff Haynes asked Peter Schuyler if the neighbors felt that their concerns were being heard and he said that they were. Peter Schuyler noted that a neighbor is requesting that the Village of Croton be responsible for plowing snow on the new sidewalk that will be installed by the applicant. Dan O'Connor suggested that Mr. Schuyler draft a letter on behalf of the homeowner to the Village Manager requesting that the Village add this area to their snow plow schedule.

The applicant wanted to mention that they found nine drains that come out in four different locations that were not on the plans and they will have to do some water control.

Geoff Haynes asked if not installing the two retaining walls will create any new steep slope concerns, and Dan O'Connor said no, that it will still be consistent with the slope that is already there. Geoff Haynes also asked how the proposed changes will affect the people who live there and Peter Schuyler responded that the applicant is taking out some trees, but will plant new trees. Peter Schuyler stated that the fence that is proposed is necessary. Eva Thaddeus asked if the proposed fence will provide a visibility barrier and it reported that yes, it will. Eva Thaddeus asked if these changes were reassuring to the neighbors and Peter Schuyler stated that it is hard to visualize.

Keith Libolt would like to wait until the buildings are framed before planting the new trees.

Steve Krisky made a motion to call for a public hearing for the amended site plan application for the property located at 41-51 Maple Street (aka "The Katz Property"), seconded by John Ghegan and the motion carried, 5-0.

   c) Croton Hudson Distillery, LLC - 1 Croton Point Ave. - Change of Use Application

David McCallen was present in support of the application. The applicant obtained a special permit from the village board to establish a business office and small rectification operation at 1 Croton Point Ave., in the Light Industrial zoning district. The application stated that the production is simple, he will have no more than three employees in the first six months of business. The applicant is applying for a state license and has a federal license. Nothing has changed since the last time he was before the planning board.

Geoff Haynes made a motion to approve the change of use application for Croton Hudson Distillery, seconded by Eva Thaddeus and the motion carried, 5 to 0.

RESOLUTION

WHEREAS, the Planning Board approved the Baltic Estates (Westwind Subdivision) on July 25, 1995; and

WHEREAS, each lot in the Baltic Estates Subdivision had a building envelope defined for it, beyond which no construction of primary buildings could occur without approval of the Planning Board; and

WHEREAS, Carl and Susan Guida, owners of Lot #10 in the Baltic Estates Subdivision (119 Scenic Drive West - Sec. 67.10 Blk. 2 Lot 4.10) have requested an expansion of that lot's building envelope for the purpose of building a larger rear deck to replace the existing rear deck; and

WHEREAS, the proposed new rear deck extends approximately two feet outside the approved building envelope, and

WHEREAS, this proposal is considered a Type II Action under the State Environmental Quality Review Act (SEQRA), therefore, no Negative Declaration is required; and

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the modification of the building envelope on Lot #10, as shown on drawings prepared by ARQ Architecture, P.C. dated March 28, 2023, is hereby approved with the following conditions:

1. Erosion control measures be installed, to the satisfaction of the Village Engineer.

Unless a building permit is issued, and work is commenced and diligently prosecuted within three years of the date of the resolution approving the building envelope expansion this approval shall become null and void. Any application for an extension of this approval shall be made six months prior to the expiration date.

The Planning Board of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson, New York

Robert Luntz, Chairman
John Ghegan
Geoffrey Haynes
Steven Krisky
Eva Thaddeus

A motion to approve the resolution, with conditions, was made John Ghegan, seconded by Steve Krisky and the motion carried by a vote of 5 to 0.

Resolution approved at the meeting held on Tuesday, April 18th, 2023.

RESOLUTION

WHEREAS, the Planning Board approved an amended Minor Site Plan application on July 14th, 2020 for John Boulos, hereafter known as "the Applicant," for a property on a vacant lot on Piney Point Avenue and designated on the Tax Map of the Village of Croton on Hudson as Section 79.13 Block 4 Lot 44; and

WHEREAS, the applicant submitted a request to modify some of the site plan requirements as shown on the approved site plans and this request was discussed by the Planning Board on February 7th and April 18th, 2023, and

WHEREAS, on February 11th, 2023, the Planning Board conducted a site visit with the applicant, Village Engineer, and LaBella Associates (formerly known as Chazen) to review the existing site conditions; and

WHEREAS, the Planning Board retained the services of LaBella Associates to review engineering reports from the applicant's geotechnical engineer, Pillori Associates, P.A., on the removal of the rock fall protection netting and LaBella Associates agreed that based on the soil conditions, the rock fall protective netting could be removed; and

WHEREAS, John A. Imbiano, Landscape Architect, provided a letter dated April 13, 2023 stating that the plantings installed at this project were installed as per the approved plans with the one exception that six trees were eliminated due to the existing bamboo that provides an adequate year-round screen, and

WHEREAS, the Planning Board members, on their own time, visited the site to see a "mock up" of an alternate covering on the outside of the parking structure and,

WHEREAS, the applicant has requested the flowing amendments to the approved Minor Site Plan for the home built on this property:

   - Elimination of the requirement to install lattice around a portion of the steel superstructure as shown on the approved architectural plans.
   - A change in landscaping plan to accept the currently planted landscaping.
   - Elimination of the rockfall protection netting.
   - Elimination of the stone veneer on the outside of the parking structure.

NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED, that the requested changes to the Minor Site Plan, be approved as follows:

   - The requirement to install lattice around a portion of the steel superstructure as shown on the approved architectural plans is eliminated.

   - The currently planted landscaping is accepted as the approved landscaping plan based on the certification letter submitted by John A. Imbiano, Landscape Architect, and dated April 13, 2023. It was stated in the letter that the existing bamboo provides an adequate year-round screen and buffer along the east property making the spruce trees unnecessary. This is conditioned on a two year warranty for the landscaping be provided and that that there be an 18 month check up on the new plantings as specified in approved landscape plan in order that the two-year warranty for the plantings can be enforced (as per condition #8 of the Planning Board's July 14, 20 resolution granting site plan approval).

   - The rockfall protection netting as shown on the approved site plan can be eliminated based on a letter, dated March 15, 2023, from the applicant's geotechnical engineer, Pillori Associates, P.A. and a letter, dated March 16, 2023, from the Planning Board's consulting geotechnical engineer, LaBella Associates, P.C. where both letters indicated that the rockfall protection netting is not required for slope stability and can be eliminated from the site plan requirements. The slopes shall have rolled erosion control blankets installed to prevent erosion.

   - The stone veneer on the outside of the parking structure as shown on the approved plans can be eliminated provided that the wood veneer currently installed on the outside left side of the parking structure also be installed on the right and rear sides of the parking structure from the top of the parking structure to the top of the steel beam and a cap or copping be installed at the top of the structure and over the steel beam.

   - All other conditions of the original Minor Site Plan Approval resolution dated July 14th, 2020, shall remain in force.

The Planning Board of the Village of
Croton-on-Hudson, New York

John Ghegan
Geoffrey Haynes
Steve Krisky
Eva Thaddeus

Motion to approve by John Ghegan, seconded by Eva Thaddeus, and carried by a vote of 5 to 0.
