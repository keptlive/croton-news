Quick updates/holdovers/follow-ups/status:
- Due to the pandemic, Croton’s Earth Day event was cancelled for this year.
- food scrap recycling: Joel pitched to C100 the option for its people to take over the paperwork
aspect of the pilot project for which we got a grant last year. C100’s leaders were receptive and
wanted details. Joel also sent them a link to an upcoming webinar on food scrap drop-off programs.
- EV/EVSE: COVID has pushed back NYSERDA/DEC program timelines. None of our activities are
affected, unless the Village seeks a grant for another EV. Regarding EV vouchers presently
available for trucks and buses, comments at a recent NYSERDA EV webinar indicate there are no
plans to raise their value, which is too low to bother pursuing them.
Len reviewed his continued work on the federal CMAQ program, which could provide additional
funding for EV school buses, and his discussions with Seth Leitman, SW’s new transportation
manager. CMAQ $ goes to Metropolitan Planning Organizations (MPO’s). Westchester County
represents us on this region’s MPO. To secure $ for an EV school bus, Croton would need to “get
in line” with others competing for such funds, and MTC would need to make funding of EV school
buses a priority. Seth Leitman of SW stated he planned to raise that option to the County
Executive’s staff, but COVID has delayed that effort. SW is also working on CMAQ to move it up its
own chain of command, with the possibility of SW having its own EV bus program with CMAQ
funds. Len felt that this process was not likely to yield any funding for Croton this year. Len will
follow up on this during the next week.
One way to get an EV for the schools might be what Virginia Beach’s (Va.) high school did: use
one for its drivers’ education program. Their EV and its charger were secured under an energy
performance contract for school facilities (Croton’s schools are involved in such a contract). To
assess switching our high school’s driver ed car to an EV, Lindsay queried PAS Auto School
(contractor running Croton’s program) on the car it now uses, annual mileage, possible $ saving on
fees, etc. We await its response.

To assess the differences between the online calculator that may be used by C100’s proposed
carbon footprinting app and the EPA’s standard online calculator, Dave reported on how they

- NYPA 100% renewable power offering: While still being developed, NYPA plans in coming months
to provide green power to its customers. We could seek it for our municipal facilities and schools,
cutting their footprints by ~30%-40%. Extra cost for our municipal power would be between $2K$10K/yr (depending on REC pricing), which could be paid out of rent money from the DPW solar
plant. REC pricing recently fell to less than 1 mill ($.001) per kWh, making fixed price green power
essentially the same cost as standard “brown” power. To see the impact if the Harmon train yard
(also a NYPA customer) switched to it, Lindsay submitted a FOIL request to the MTA on that
facility. To avoid overlap or confusion, Joel will ask C100 to exclude NYPA customers from its
Promoting Green Power program.

Research Project – Questions Concerning the “Community Grant Program” (“CGP”) of
the New York State Pollution Prevention Institute (“NYSP2I”)
April 22, 2020
~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~

Research Question 1 – Can a CGP grant be issued for air pollution reductions from vehicles (e.g., by using
EV school buses)?
Answer – Education programs concerning the wisdom/benefits of EV school buses could conceivably
receive funding. Applying grant money to the purchase of an EV school bus would be prohibited. CGP
funds may not be used for purchases of “equipment”, which is defined as any item having a useful life
of 1 year or more and costing more than $1,000 per unit. Grant sizes are also limited to a maximum of
$20,000; and
Research Question 2 - Is the educational component of the criteria to be met for CGP funding limited in any
way, (e.g., is it mandatory to focus on greenhouse gas reduction?)
Answer – Projects do not have to be focused directly on greenhouse gas emissions reduction. The
educational component of the CGP is broader. To clarify the scope of educational topics suitable for
grant funding, NYSP2I’s website materials make reference to the US EPA’s definition of “Pollution
Prevention” (https://www.epa.gov/p2/learn-about-pollution-prevention).
Education program proposals focused on educating the public about water cleanliness or composting, for
example, could be awarded a grant.

Research Question 3 – What is the confirmed deadline, and what are forms that must be followed for
complete an CGP grant application?
Answer – May 29, 2020 at 5:00 p.m. is the filing deadline. This is the link for the downloadable

Application rules specify both a required and optional component for document submissions. Required
documents are downloadable (described below), and include an Application Form and a budget
template. A detailed project description is required, and limited to 12 pages. Optional documents are
resumes and letters of support. Applicants must submit a W-9 form, but applicant entities are not
required to have an IRS “tax-exempt approval as a 501(c)(3) charitable/public service organization.
NYSP2I – Details
The NYSP2I was launched in 2008 on the campus of Rochester Institute of Technology (RIT) through funding
by Empire State Development (ESD). The NYSP2I was established to “give businesses, nonproﬁts,
municipalities, and communities across the Empire State the resources and expertise they need to realize
the beneﬁts of sustainability”. As a pilot program with a budget of $500,000, the NYSP2I’s first initiative was
to provide technical support to manufacturers across New York State. Today its budget exceeds $4 million
and it works across many sectors and within many communities throughout the state without being limited
only to manufacturing companies. NYSP2I is sponsored by New York State’s Department of Environmental

NYSP2I - Community Grants Program:
The Community Grants Program (CGP) supports initiatives that promote public awareness, understanding,
and implementation of pollution prevention practices at the local level. Typical grant awards range from
$10,000 to $20,000 per project, and the maximum grant award is $20,000 per project.
Submitted proposals must meet the following criteria and guidelines to be considered:
 Increase awareness, understanding and/or lead to implementation of pollution prevention practices and/or
behaviors at the community or municipal level
 Demonstrate a clear and actionable project plan (see ‘Application Format’ for project plan requirements in
Section VII)
 Be able to measure program outcomes
 Result in one or more concrete deliverable(s) (past examples include training or public education
workshops; demonstration projects; implementation of a new policy, technology, or practice; and creation
and dissemination of brochures, videos or a website)
 Be completed in accordance with the budget guidelines provided in Section IV ‘Budget Guidelines, Size of
Awards, and Distribution of Funds’
 Be completed within the 2020-2021 project timeline, which is October 1, 2020 through September 30,
2021.
Projects Given Preference Are Those That:
 utilize partnerships and collaborations with other organizations
 demonstrate a broad community impact
 address populations and communities affected by, experiencing or susceptible to environmental harms and
risk
 can easily be replicated and used by other organizations
 demonstrate an ability to source funding from additional sources
 are located in Environmental Justice (EJ) communities
Who Can Apply for the Grant? Community organizations, local government agencies, municipal
departments, and other public sector and nonprofit entities based in NYS can apply for CGP funds. The
definition of “Community Organization” is as follows:
Any non-profit group based in New York State that conducts the majority of its work within the state.
This includes, but is not limited to:
 lawfully incorporated/registered organizations focused on environmental, public health, or
occupational health protection;
 academic institutions;
 neighborhood associations, health centers; and environmental justice organizations;
 community development corporations;
 public benefit corporations;
 local unions and labor organizations; and “other nonprofits”.
Definition of “Local government agencies”:
“Offices or departments of a city, town, county, or primary and secondary schools in New York State”.
This includes, but is not limited to:
 Local environmental agencies,
 Boards of health,
 Departments of public works,
