Public Hearing #2
A motion to open a Public Hearing on Local Law Introductory No. 13 of
2023 to amend Chapter 66, entitled Adult Entertainment Uses, of the
Village Code to update listed definitions for Adult Entertainment
Cabaret was made by Trustee Horowitz. The motion was seconded by
Trustee Nicholson and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Village Attorney Whitehead explained that this particular provision in
the Adult Entertainment law that included male and female
impersonators dates back to the mid 1980’s. Village Attorney
Whitehead stated that in today’s world it has been deemed
inappropriate to include male and female impersonators in that
category because male and female impersonators have been deemed a
form of expression which is protected as free speech under the
Constitution of the United States. Village Attorney Whitehead stated
that she understands there are some concerns regarding protecting
children, but there are other laws that protect against obscenities, and
it would be a separate issue and would be addressed differently. Village
Attorney Whitehead stated that we believe that if we left this in our
law, it would be problematic for the Village under the free speech
provisions in our Constitution and therefore we feel it is appropriate to
remove it at this time.
Ed Riely, 110 Truesdale Drive, Croton on Hudson, stated that he has
concerns regarding the exploitation of children and believes this is a
form of adult entertainment. Mr. Riely stated that if the law is approved
and we allow drag queen story hours, the Village should require
background checks similar to what the Village does for anyone working
with children.
Alanna Fraase, Radnor Avenue, Croton on Hudson, stated that she is
pleased with the Board’s response to this law and supports their
immediate action that aligns with the Village’s statements of inclusivity
and its mission to foster a welcoming diverse community.

Andy Derudo, Emerson Avenue, Croton on Hudson, stated that he is in

Chris Hogan stated that she works in Croton and thanked the

not reflect today’s standards and viewpoints.

DISCUSSION:
Trustee Simon thanked everyone for their comments and emphasized that what
we are trying to do this evening is to get these four words (male or female
impersonators) out of the current statute. Trustee Simon stated that there are
other forums and other opportunities in the future where we can talk about
some of the other issues that were raised and perhaps there is a role for local
government and perhaps there isn’t but, in this instance, we are, on advice of
our Legal Counsel and based on common sense, removing these words out of
our current statute. Trustee Simon stated that he raised his children during the
1980’s-1990’s and we did not have to deal with a lot of these issues, it is a
different time, and we have to acknowledge that there have been certain
evolutions in our society and believes this is the appropriate thing for the Board
to do and is pleased to support this.
Trustee Gallelli stated that it behooves us as Trustees to take the advice of our
Legal Counsel and she personally supports the proposed amendment.
Trustee Nicholson thanked those who came out this evening, as well as those
that emailed and wrote letters to the newspaper. Trustee Nicholson stated that
she believes the right thing to do tonight is to revise our current Law, and as we
go forward, we need to continue to review our Code and question the necessity
of an Adult Cabaret Law in this day and age. Trustee Nicholson stated that we
are here to represent our Village residents and to make sure that our Code is
relevant and lawful. Trustee Nicholson stated that the bottom line is that parents
have the right to choose how their children are taught values and they have the
right to make the decision if drag story hour is appropriate for their child.
Trustee Horowitz stated that she is grateful to everyone who came out this
evening and shared their views on this issue, it goes deep, and it is painful, and
we have to always speak out for the truth.
Mayor Pugh also thanked everyone for speaking this evening and expressed his
gratitude to his colleagues on the Board for their thoughts. Mayor Pugh stated
that he does not feel that we should continue an official endorsement of a value
system that does not reflect the values of this community.

Resolution 160 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE GALLELLI, seconded by TRUSTEE SIMON, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton- onHudson, New York, with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS the Volunteer Fire Department Length of Service Award Program
(“LOSAP”) was approved by referendum in 2003, and
WHEREAS Article 11-a of the General Municipal Law (“GML”) authorizes the
Village, as LOSAP Sponsor, to amend the LOSAP, and
WHEREAS a qualified volunteer firefighter currently earns a $20 monthly Service
Award for each year of Service Credit, payable at the age of 60, and
WHEREAS under the current LOSAP provisions, the maximum number of years
of Service Credit a qualified volunteer firefighter may earn is 20 years, which
represents a maximum monthly Service Award of $400, and
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees wishes to authorize amendments to the
LOSAP for the benefit of qualified volunteer firefighters and to authorize a public
referendum with respect thereto, and
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees proposes to amend the LOSAP, effective
January 1, 2024, to extend the maximum number of years of Service Credit for
which a qualified volunteer firefighter may earn a Service Award to 40 years and
increase the monthly Service Award to $30, which represents a maximum
monthly Service Award of $1,200, and
WHEREAS this amendment is consistent with the intent of the LOSAP to recruit,
retain and reward volunteer firefighters,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees, as
authorized by Article 11-A of the GML, approves an amendment to the LOSAP to
extend the maximum years of Service Credit that may be earned under its
LOSAP from 20 to 40 years and to increase the monthly Service Award from $20
to $30, increasing the maximum projected monthly Service Award from $400 to
$1,200, effective January 1, 2024. The annual funding cost of the current
program for 2023 was approximately $120,000 ($1,034 per participating
volunteer). The current cost of administration is about $7,200 per year. The

Village Board of Trustees

to be about $220,000 ($1,897 for participating Volunteer). There will be no
increase in the program administration costs as a result of this Amendment. The

presently in effect, and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that this resolution is subject to a mandatory
referendum of qualified voters of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson and said

Resolution 161 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE SIMON, seconded by TRUSTEE GALLELLI, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York, with a 5-0 vote.
WHEREAS a natural resources inventory (“NRI”) compiles maps and descriptions
of natural areas and provides a reference for planning in a community, and
WHEREAS the purpose of an NRI is to provide information for comprehensive
land use and conservation planning, and to allow natural resource information to
be included in local planning and zoning, and
WHEREAS the Town of Cortlandt and the Village of Croton-on-Hudson are
interested in working together on an intermunicipal NRI for both communities,
and
WHEREAS the NYSDEC Estuary Grant Program is offering a local stewardship
planning opportunity for municipalities to create a basic NRI, at no cost to the
Village of Croton-on-Hudson, but requiring a significant volunteer contribution,

Village Board of Trustees
June 26, 2023
Page 20

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board hereby supports the
submission of a grant application to NYSDEC to create the natural resources
inventory, and
AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Village Board authorizes the Town of
Cortlandt to serve as the lead for this NRI.
DISCUSSION
Village Manager Healy advised that the Town of Cortlandt is serving as the lead
applicant and the Town’s Director of Planning has offered to put everything
together, our Conservation Advisory Council Chair, Janet Monahan, has offered
to coordinate the volunteer aspects.
