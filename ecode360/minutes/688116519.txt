Village Manager Healy stated that based on the discussion that took
place at the August 22, 2022, work session, Village Engineer Daniel
O’Connor identified approximately 816 parcels with a higher Town
assessment than Village assessment, of these parcels, approximately
83% are single-family homes, 8% are two- or three-family homes and
the remaining 11% are other categories (commercial, vacant land,

apartment buildings), these properties would likely see an increase in
the Village portion of their total tax bill (approximately 30%),
approximately 2,058 parcels were identified with a higher Village
assessment than Town assessment and these properties would likely
see a decrease in the Village portion of their total tax bill
(approximately 30%). In terms of dollars and cents, the Village’s
average assessment is 15,800, if an assessment were to go up by
10%, a 2022-2023 Village tax bill would increase by $439.93.
Likewise, if an assessment was to go down by 10%, a 2022-2023
Village tax bill would decrease by $439.93, it is important to
remember that 70% of a property’s total tax bill is already generated
from the Town assessment roll, this includes Town, County and School
taxes, it is also important to remember that if a resident feels their
property is assessed incorrectly, they can annually file a grievance
with the Assessor and present their case on Grievance Day, currently,
a resident needs to attend two separate Grievance Day hearings (one
in February for the Village and one in June for the Town), if the Village
were to cease being an assessing unit, there would only be one
Grievance Day for all tax purposes. Village Manager Healy stated that
he will need a decision this evening whether to terminate the position
or begin the process of looking for a part-time Assessor because an
Assessment Roll will need to be done.
Trustee Simon asked how the Town of Cortlandt is coming along in
finding someone to fill that position and if we chose to keep our own
Assessing position what does that pool of candidates look like.
Village Manager Healy stated that the Town hired a retired Assessor
from the Village of Scarsdale that is serving on a temporary basis, it is
not easy to find an Assessor, in speaking with Town they were
anticipating it taking up to two years to permanently fill the vacancy.
Trustee Gallelli stated that it is important for people to understand that
sixty to seventy percent of their tax bill is already based on the Town
of Cortlandt’s valuation. Trustee Gallelli asked if we could characterize
these 817 parcels that would see a higher assessment and asked if

Village Manager Healy stated that the Assessment Roll needs to be
updated every year, and those residents do have a recourse and can
grieve their assessment.
Mayor Pugh stated that we should not look at this as “classes” of
properties, it is case specific and assessments will be made on a case
by case basis, the overwhelming majority of someone’s tax bill is
dependent on the Town Assessment that determines the taxation for
School, Town and County taxes, so if someone’s Village tax bills is
raised as result of having a single assessment then they should grieve
their School, Town and County taxes as well.
Trustee Simon stated that it is a conversation worth having but
believes that Trustee Gallelli’s point about a deeper analysis would be
helpful, and while it may take some time to complete, he believes that
we should go forward with hiring an Assessor for the Village and is not
comfortable giving up a governmental function at this time, when the
Town does hire a permanent Assessor, we can look at it and determine
at that point whether or not we want to consolidate or share that
service.
Mayor Pugh stated that he does not share that view, we are looking at
all taxpayers having to pay for an Assessor for the benefit of the
minority of taxpayers and there is a remedy if they are being Assessed
unfairly.
The consensus of the Trustees was to find an Assessor.
Village Manager Healy stated that he will reach out to Attorney Staudt
for recommendations, and we will try to get someone as soon as
possible.

regulations for that area to encourage residential development, and

resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Crotonon-Hudson, New York with a 5-0 vote.

from New York State for the acquisition of electric vehicle charging stations at the
Croton-Harmon Train Station, and
WHEREAS an appraisal of existing parking spaces is required for this process, and

NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board authorizes the Village
Treasurer to transfer $2,500 from Contingency Account A1990.4000 to the
following account:
A1940.4000 – Survey of Land: $2,500
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Simon stated that our Sustainability Committee has been working on a
grant that could bring about significant electric vehicle charging capacity at our
train station.
