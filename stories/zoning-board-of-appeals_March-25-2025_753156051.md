# Croton Resident Granted Fence Variance Due to Steep Slope

### Zoning Board approves variance for Mount Airy Road South homeowner after neighbor dispute over fence height.

The Zoning Board of Appeals (ZBA) granted a variance for a fence at 20 Mount Airy Road South, after a neighbor raised concerns about its height. The applicant, Rachel Kirschner, explained that due to the steep slope of her yard, a standard six-foot fence installed in a "step style" resulted in portions exceeding the height limit.

Kirschner told the board she had tried to address the issue by cutting some panels. She said her neighbor never contacted her directly, but instead complained to the fence installer, then erected their own fence abutting hers.

Board members noted the fence was in the rear yard, "open on top," and that Kirschner had made efforts to lessen the impact. "It was also noted by the Board that they felt the applicant had put forth her best effort in remediating and lessening the issue, as well as being sensitive to the neighbor, but again was limited due to the steep slope of the lot."

The board unanimously approved the variance of up to one foot for portions of the fence exceeding six feet.

# Cleveland Drive Resident OK'd for Front Porch Variance

### Zoning Board approves variance for a small portion of a front porch on Cleveland Drive.

The Zoning Board of Appeals approved a front yard variance for the property at 189 Cleveland Drive. The applicant, Laura Perdomo-Cohen, sought the variance to allow for the construction of an open-air front porch as part of a larger home improvement project that includes a second-story addition.

Architect Michael Bevivino explained that due to the "radiused" nature of the lot, a small 10.8 square foot corner of the porch would encroach 1 foot 4 inches into the front yard setback. He noted a similar porch existed directly across the street at 190 Cleveland Drive.

The board agreed the request was minimal and that no other feasible options existed for creating a usable front porch. The board also acknowledged a letter of support from neighbors. The variance was approved unanimously.

# Grand Street Homeowner Receives Approval for Pavilion Enclosure

### ZBA greenlights side and rear yard variances for enclosure of existing pavilion, despite neighbor concerns.

Adriana Franggi, owner of 339 Grand Street, received unanimous approval from the Zoning Board of Appeals for side and rear yard variances to enclose an existing attached pavilion. Franggi had previously been granted variances for the pavilion itself in January. This new request was required because enclosing the structure with windows would increase its non-conformity with village code.

Assistant Village Engineer Ron Wegner clarified that the enclosure would be considered a sunroom, not a finished livable space, and would not increase the home's square footage.

Board members discussed a neighbor who had objected to the original pavilion, but noted the neighbor's driveway was closest to the structure and that they had been unable to reach her regarding the current request. Another neighbor had verbally supported the home improvements. The board determined the enclosure would not negatively impact the neighborhood and would decrease sound from the pavilion's use.
