Motion to open a Public Hearing on Local Law Introductory 4 of 2021 to
amend Chapter 197 of the Village Code entitled “Streets and
Sidewalks”, to include an appeals process for the removal of snow and
ice on sidewalks was made by Trustee Horowitz. Motion was seconded by
Trustee Simon and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Village Attorney Whitehead explained this this adds an appeal process if
someone feels that they have been incorrectly charged.
Peter Schuyler, no address given, suggested issuing an appearance ticket
and having it adjudicated by the Village Court similar to other Zoning
Code violations.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that this is not a Zoning Code provision,
this is a public safety issue and simply bringing someone into court does
not address the snow and ice issue on sidewalks; if a homeowner does not
do it, the Village can have the work done and charge the homeowner for
that expenditure.
Mr. Schuyler asked how someone further appeals if they are not happy
with the Manager’s adjudication and how does the Village determine the
amount that is due.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that if a homeowner does not agree
they could take it to court; the costs are based on the costs to the Village
to have the work done.
Written Comment:
Marc Albrecht, 191 Cleveland Drive, Croton on Hudson, states in his e-mail
that it is appropriate for the Village to offer an appeal process but
suggested that since the Village Manager is required to maximize the
Village’s finances the Village should identify an unbiased individual or
Panel to make the final determination.
Village Manager King stated that the Village Manager’s job is not just to
maximize fees, but to be responsible to its residents, when a decision is
made it is based on facts that are given to the Village Manager along with

Mayor Pugh stated that we want students to be able to walk safely to

Bryan Healy explained that the purpose of the Public Hearing is to hear
from residents within the “low-mod” area of the Village which consists of
the area from “Brook Street” to “Maple Street”, down to “South
Riverside”, as well as “Clinton Street” and the “Wayne Street”
neighborhood. Mr. Healy advised that the first project that was submitted
to the County were improvements to “Dobbs Park”, we are looking to
replace the basketball court and playground equipment; the playground
equipment was installed over twenty years ago and is beyond its useful
life; we have received estimates of approximately $440,000 for this
project of which half will be covered by the Village and half, if we are
successful, by the Grant; the second project is the “Brook Street Parking
Lot” (a small lot at the west end of “Brook Street”), it is currently
unpaved and not adequately sized for the amount of vehicles that are
currently in that neighborhood and Westchester County has developed a
concept plan to double the existing spaces from nine to eighteen that will
help to get vehicles of the street; the budget for that project is $240,000
with half being covered by the Village and half by the Grant, our third
project is the retaining wall on “lower Grant Street”, the wall is leaning
significantly and we have received an estimate of $73,000 to remove the
existing wall and replace it with a new wall, the estimate does not include
any tree or sidewalk work that would need to be done, and once again the
Village would be paying for half of the project with the Grant covering the
other half. Mr. Healy explained that the sidewalk along South Riverside
between Oneida and Bungalow Road cannot be included in this program
because that sidewalk falls just outside the “Low mod” area, but we are
hoping to have that re-done through another avenue.
Trustee Simon stated that the projects that have been selected really go
to the heart of the intention of the “CDBG” program and going through
the County is an excellent process.
Trustee Gallelli stated that “Dobbs Park” and the “Brook Street Parking
Lot” are clearly projects that are on Village owned properties and asked
Mr. Healy to explain the “Grand Street Wall” project where it may appear
not to be on Village property.
Bryan Healy explained that the wall is right on the Village’s right-of-way
area and is actually helping to support the roadway which makes it an
