Correspondence Between Matt and Michael Balter

April 22, 2026
The following is the complete, unedited correspondence between Matthew Broudy (croton.news) and Michael Balter (Croton Chronicle) regarding the guest editorial by Amos Bloomberg about the Croton Free Library. Published in full with permission from both parties.
Matt

I was going to publish a correction piece, but I thought about it and thought I would give you a chance to correct the record.

As far as I can tell, the Peekskill library is an association library and will remain so. Your guest writer said "Peekskill has done it," but I can't find any evidence to support that claim.

There are 11 association libraries in the Westchester Library System, which I would argue is important context when it is presented as an outlier.

Without further context, the statement that the library workers failed to unionize seems misleading. They voted to unionize and voted against the contract offered a few years later, and received pay increases and better terms during that time. I think these statements are probably irresponsible without more context or direct reporting from the library workers. No anonymous source is cited, and statements are made implying the mindset of the people working at the library. I am friends with [redacted] and have only heard her say good things, so this contradicts people's experience. It is beyond the scope of an editorial to speak for a group of people, who are probably not monolithic in their perspectives.

Also, the statement that library workers don't have free speech is exaggerated. They have the same protections any of us have, just not the additional civil protections.

The initial argument is flawed. Just because a document about different structures for public libraries mentions sustainability issues with association libraries does not imply that they are necessarily bad — just that the article is about frameworks for public libraries. This is misleading and makes me suspect that some of the underlying research may have been driven by AI, or at least a person researching to support a supposition, not doing honest research. It is not an honest representation of the source material, as it was not designed to contrast public libraries with association libraries.

Just wanted to offer you my two cents.

As an aside, I dislike the association structure of the library. It is a shame this discussion has been presented this way to the community, especially when the library is looking to expand. This is a good opportunity to responsibly advocate for change to the legal structure of the library.

Michael Balter

Hi Matt,

I've looked over what you have written here and these are my thoughts.

I really can't take seriously the idea that your news site is going to serve as some kind of backup fact-check operation for the Chronicle. The Chronicle is not perfect — no news outlet is — but your own site continues to feature routine errors, right down to the spelling of names, which I know you were working on ("Mickey Weiss" instead of the correct Micci Weiss, for example — I assume using phonetic spelling).

Also, you have acknowledged that your AI-driven site includes the Chronicle among your sources, but you don't seem ready to admit how much that is true. Your site repeats exclusive copyrighted content that originates with the Chronicle, and your human overlay is obviously very influenced by the Chronicle's interpretations. As you know, there is a lot of litigation going on — settled, for example the Anthropic case, and ongoing — that puts some AI operations at legal risk.

As for Amos Bloomberg's guest editorial, which is not original Chronicle reporting but an opinion piece: when he first submitted it, I questioned some things and asked him to provide detailed links to sources, which he did. He turned out to be right and I wrong about a couple of things. But of course there could be errors in his piece that I did not catch.

If so, in my view, the most appropriate way for you to comment on things you think are inaccurate is in the Comments section of the Chronicle. Of course you are free to publish some kind of "Correcting the Record" piece on croton.news, but I am not sure that is the most collegial way to go about it — especially given the ambiguous legal situation of at least some of the content in your own publication, which up to now I have chosen to overlook.

Best,
Michael
