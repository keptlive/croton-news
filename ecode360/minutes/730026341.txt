Resolution 100 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ, seconded by TRUSTEE GALLELLI, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-onHudson, New York with a 4-0 vote.
WHEREAS the New York State Legislature established the Senior Citizen Rent
Increase Exemption (SCRIE) program in 1970 to protect low-income tenants aged
sixty-two and over from certain rent increases in rent controlled and rent
stabilized apartments, and
WHEREAS in October 2005 the New York State Legislature established the Disability
Rent Increase Exemption (DRIE) program, which protects eligible disabled tenants
from rent increases as well, and
WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson finds it in the public interest to adopt
legislation in accordance with the enabling legislation enacted by the State
Legislature with respect to tax abatements of real property taxes for senior citizens
and disabled persons living in rent-regulated/rent-controlled properties, and
WHEREAS Local Law Introductory No. 8 of 2023 has been drafted for such
purposes, and
WHEREAS a Public Hearing was opened and closed on May 8, 2023,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby
adopts Local Law Introductory No. 8 of 2023 to amend Article VIII, Chapter 204 of
the Village Code, Tax Abatement for Rent-Controlled and Rent-Regulated Property
occupied by Certain Senior Citizens or Persons with Disabilities, which upon
adoption becomes Local Law 8 of 2023.

7. PUBLIC COMMENTS ON AGENDA ITEMS
Joel Gingold, 55 Nordica Drive, Croton on Hudson, stated that the
Manager’s memo does not address the question of visitors and suggested
reaching out to other communities to see how it was handled, it would also

Village Board of Trustees

be interesting to see how many of the sixty-three tickets issued last year
were issued to residents and their guests. Mr. Gingold stated that a time
change in the evening would be helpful, but he does not believe it gets to
the heart of the problem, the current restrictions on weekends and
holidays are a nuisance and needs to be modified especially with respect to
visitors, the questions in the survey are fine but does think they are
complete and should be posted on the Village’s website. Mr. Gingold
commended the Village staff for the job they did on the pump house on

Mayor Pugh stated that even under a Residential Parking Permit System it
would not be possible to accommodate guests, the Village could look into a
permit system that would require some form of a paid permit or a free
pre-reservation system, but those are things that would need to be
investigated further.
Village Manager Healy stated that he has looked at other Residential
Parking Permit Systems in Westchester, and none have provisions for
guests because they are not permitted under State law, what the State
allows and encourages communities to do is create time limit parking.
Village Attorney Whitehead stated that every community that has adopted
Residential Parking Permit Systems has struggled with the issue of guests
and visitors and there is no simple answer.
