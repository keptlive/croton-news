4. PUBLIC HEARINGS:
Public Hearing #1
A Motion to continue a Public Hearing to consider the special permit application
from WBP Development LLC for the construction of a multifamily development at 1
Croton Point Avenue was made by Trustee Murtaugh. Motion was seconded by
Trustee Politi and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Mayor Pugh went over the guidelines for holding a Public Hearing.
John Bainlardi of WBP Development gave an overview of the proposed project, all
documents pertaining to the project such as the fiscal analysis, school children
analysis, updated site plans, updated building renderings, fire apparatus study,
updated Part I EAF, and a memo with respect to the LWRP Consistency can be
found on the Village Website under the “Project Page”.

Trustee Murtaugh asked if the roadway that they anticipate the school buses would
be using is the roadway that Metro North has an easement with the Village to
access their property.
Mr. Banlardi advised that the Village granted the MTA an easement so they can
access their storage yard.
Village Manager Healy answered the following questions.
Financial Liability – the property currently generates $53,219 for all taxing
jurisdictions; under the proposed development the property would generate
$407,546 ($120,146 would come to the Village). For comparison purposes, the
current tax bill for the Van Wyck apartments) is $32,789.
Traffic Study- we are still awaiting additional information, but we do know that
additional traffic generated by the proposed building would add less than a one
second delay in travel time, and there would not be any changes in the level of
service for the motoring public, there are currently six hundred less cars going to
the train station than there were in 2019.
School Enrollment – School enrollment peaked between 2009-2010 and it is down
190 students since that peak, but we are waiting for an updated enrollment study,
For the 2024-25 School District Budget included a 2.14 percent increase which was
withing the Tax Cap and reminded everyone that the Village Assessment does not
determine the school tax, the Town of Cortlandt is the assessing entity.
Green Spaces – this is an ongoing conversation that the Village is having with the
developer, the RFP called for some kind of public amenity but that would be part of
the Site Plan review process.
Visual Impact – will be looked at as part of the SEQRA review, impacts on private
properties are addressed in the Village Code and will be discussed during the Site
Plan review process.
Market Rate Condominiums – The Village did not receive any proposals for market
rate condominiums.
Public Safety - The Police Department has reviewed the plan and had no concerns
to share with the developer, EMS was only concerned that stretchers would fit in
the elevator, and we have confirmation that they would, the Fire Department
made a number of suggestions that the developer is working on, the building will
be fully sprinklered as per Village Code, there are also multiple pathways that are

being investigated with respect to providing water service to the building, the
preferred option is to extend the water main.
Mayor Pugh made the following comments:
-the Village of Croton has been designated a “Pro-Housing” community, and even
with the units already approved, including LOT A, the Village is not averaging a 1%
annual growth rate,
-the Zoning for LOT A is for a 5-story building, and per the SEQRA Review there
are no discernible impacts for the general public, there are some potential impacts
to privately owned parcels and while there are protections in the Village Code as it
relates to views, that would be under the Site Plan review process
-the inclusion of the parcel adjacent to LOT A doubled the area size and capping
the number of units is not a reasonable goal and is a policy decision
-this project is seeking State funding to create affordable condominiums which are
funded by taxpayers throughout the State, it would be illegal to restrict it to
Croton residents, residents and non-residents will have an equal chance of finding
housing but if no such developments are built in our community, there would be
fewer opportunities for residents
-the Village is not being asked to solve the housing problems on our own, there
are more than 200 Pro-Housing communities in New York State with more applying
and all those communities have committed to taking steps to achieve an average
1% annual growth in housing supply, it is a voluntary incentive approach that the
New York Conference of Mayors proposed in response to the Governors housing
plan and this is how we maintain local control and progress in a way that is best
for our community, it also gives the Village access to a pool of $650,000 million in
State funding grants.
Attorney Whitehead explained that there is a lot of case law on the ability to adopt
a Moratorium on development, because a Moratorium takes away common law
rights and property rights, they are looked at very carefully by the Courts, and
they cannot be arbitrary, Courts have deemed Moratoriums valid if they are
enacted in response to a dire necessity, if the Board’s action is reasonably
calculated to alleviate or prevent a crises condition and if the municipality is taking
steps to rectify the problem such as amending their Zoning or updating a
Comprehensive Plan, the time of the Moratorium must be reasonable and the

Board is not in the position where it could legally adopt a Moratorium that would
prevent this project or any other project from moving forward.

PUBLIC HEARING #2
A motion to open a Public Hearing on Local Law Introductory No. 10 of 2024 to
amend the Zoning Code of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson in relation to
fees in lieu of parkland was made by Trustee Simon. Motion was seconded by
Trustee Nicholson and approved with a 5-0 vote.
Village Manager Healy explained that when a new development is proposed or
when property is sub-divided the developer is required to provide either parkland
on that property or if they cannot provide it because the lot is too small, they
would be required to provide a payment in Lieu of Parkland.

Fund that can be spent on future recreation land acquisitions or recreation
projects.

FLOODING & EROSION POLICIES
Buildings and other structures will be sited
in the coastal area so as to minimize
damage to property and the endangering
of human lives caused by flooding and
erosion.
Activities or development in the coastal
area will be undertaken so as to minimize
damage to natural resources and property
from flooding and erosion by protecting
natural protective features including
beaches, dunes, barrier islands and
bluffs.
Every effort should be made to protect
Croton Point, a natural protective barrier
to Croton Bay from activities or
development that would increase erosion
of or flooding of the point.
The construction or reconstruction of
erosion protection structures shall be
undertaken only if they have a reasonable
probability of controlling erosion for at
least thirty years as demonstrated in
design and construction standards and/or
assured maintenance or replacement
programs.
Activities and development, including the
construction or reconstruction of erosion
protection structures, shall be undertaken
so that there will be no measurable
increase in erosion or flooding at the site
of such activities or development, or at
other locations.
Mining, excavation or dredging in coastal
waters shall not significantly interfere with
the natural coastal processes which
supply beach materials to land adjacent to
such waters and shall be undertaken in a
manner which will not cause an increase
in erosion of such land.
Public funds shall only be used for erosion
protective structures where necessary to
protect human life, and new development
which requires a location within or
adjacent to an erosion hazard area to be
able to function, or existing development;
and only where the public benefits
outweigh the long term monetary and
other costs including the potential for
increasing erosion and adverse effects on
natural protective features.
Non-structural measures to minimize
damage to natural resources and property
from flooding and erosion shall be used
whenever possible.

and on the steep slopes inland shall be of
