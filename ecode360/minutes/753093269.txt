Announcements: On 5/30/24, Croton was re-certified as a Bronze Climate Smart
Community (CSC), getting us 800 CEC points. On 9/5/24, we were re-certified as
Silver, getting us another 600 CEC points. The re-certs are good until 9/2029. Croton
is now 3rd in the CSC program, after Tompkins County and the Village of Hastings.
As of 9/20/24, Croton still holds 1st place in NYSERDA’s CEC program with 16,200
points. Kingston has 11,600, Hastings 11,100, Bedford 10,500, and Dobbs Ferry
10,000. No other muni has over 10,000 points.
On 7/12/24, the VB renewed Croton’s CCA contract for another two years. On 8/6/24,
paperwork was filed to secure another 2,000 CEC points for doing so.

- Heat Pumps
Harmon firehouse addition: On 5/10/24, Lindsay sent Village staff the spec
developed by Jon and Dan for a hybrid rooftop ASHP. It will furnish most heat, with a
gas-fired heating coil to heat on very cold days. Our CEC grants will cover the extra
cost over that of the gas-fired A/C in the original design. Len indicated that work on
the addition should be completed before the end of 2025.
Washington firehouse garage HPs: The option for a central HP and electric boiler,
instead of individual HPs for the garage, needs review. It could fully electrify the
building, allowing for removal of its oil-fired boiler and 45-year old underground oil
tank. Via a suggestion from Dave, Lindsay found a 2/24 announcement of a packaged
system that replaces fossil-fueled hydronic boilers with a combination air-to-water heat
pump and electric boiler. Jon and Dan will be asked to review the option.
DPW HPs: The ASHRAE energy audit of the building done in April was fixed and
updated 8/28/24. It found there are rarely more than 4 people in the building at one
time (DPW has 28 employees). Some employees work remotely, while others are in
the field most of their workday. Three aging A/Cs were recommended to be replaced
by heat pumps whose operation would be staged with existing hydronic controls. Most
other measures had paybacks measured in decades.
Residential HPs: On 5/30/24, NY opened its IRA-funded rebate/voucher program for
its Home Electrification and Appliance Rebates (HEAR) Program. Only low-income
households (~$50K/yr) eligible for NY’s EmPower+ program can access it. Rollout of
the next phase (for middle-income families) is expected later this year. In early
August, Len assessed how many Croton households have annual incomes of $50K/yr
or less, pursuant to a possible HPWH campaign. See below for details.

- His analysis of 2022 data for Croton household with incomes at or below $50K/yr
found ~273. That cutoff covers those that might be eligible for recently offered
vouchers for heat pump water heaters (HPWH). While 80.1% of households are
owned in Croton, the split between owners and renters could not be determined. The
% earning $50K or less is likely much lower. At best, that would leave ~220 eligible.
Factoring those with electric water heaters (~40% at most) yields up to 88. With an atbest response rate of 10% for such efforts, fewer than 10 would be expected to
participate in a campaign to replace electric water heaters with HPWHs, too few to
initiate a program. Later this year, if the State expands the voucher option to medium
income households (between $50K and ~$133K/yr, i.e., 80% of median income), a
similar analysis will be performed.

- New Business / Other Issues
Regarding a potential conflict-of-interest if a committee advisor wanted to bid on a job
about which he had consulted, Brian and Bryan Healy got a 7/16/24 opinion from
Rebecca Ruscito, Counsel for the NY Conference of Mayors that stated:
“No – the electrical contractor (EC) would not be precluded from bidding on this
contract. The specifications cannot be drafted in a way to give preference to the contractor,
but the EC may still bid.
Another question is how the committee is selecting the EC and is the committee engaging
him as a consultant? Assuming the committee is not contracting with the EC as a
consultant and the EC is offering his opinion, there’s no professional services contract we
need to consider. But if the committee is entering into a contract with the EC as a
consultant, this contract also needs a competitive procedure before the EC can be engaged.
All that to say, service providers/vendors/etc. may be utilized to help the village draft
specifications, but the specifications cannot be drafted in a way that gives preferential
treatment to the service provider.”

Bottom line: we’re good to go as long as we don’t pay an EC and he doesn’t get to
write the actual bid specs for the job.
Following up on Dave’s suggestion for such an advisor, we approached Nick Kalyvas

design and costing for the train station EV charger project. He found several code and
