# Croton Delays Vote on Tax Assessment Consolidation After Resident Concerns

### The Board of Trustees is holding off on a decision to consolidate the Village's assessment roll with the Town of Cortlandt after hearing concerns from residents about potential tax increases.

The Village Board of Trustees has delayed a vote on Local Law Introductory No. 14 of 2024, which would consolidate the Village's tax assessment roll with the Town of Cortlandt. The decision came after a public hearing on March 12 where residents voiced concerns about potential tax increases and the loss of local control over property assessments.

Currently, the Village and Town assess properties separately, leading to discrepancies in assessed values. The proposed consolidation would eliminate the Village's assessment roll and rely solely on the Town's assessments for calculating Village property taxes.

Village Treasurer Daniel Tucker explained that while this isn't a revaluation, variations between the Village and Town assessment rolls mean that most properties in the Village will experience some type of change. Village Manager Bryan Healy added that the State is discouraging duplication of services and in the era of the Tax Cap, being able to consolidate this service is helpful.

Several residents expressed concerns about potential tax increases due to the consolidation. Michael Mamone stated that he believes the Village of Briarcliff Manor is attempting to get their Assessor back, and since we have everything in place, why fix something that is not broken. Rich Kingham noted a $100,000 discrepancy between his Village and Town assessments. David Goldman said he is very concerned because his property will receive a 55% increase in taxes.

Gary Eisinger argued that the proposal would raise taxes on over 1,200 homeowners, while lowering assessments for approximately 1,300 homeowners. He also claimed that over 200 residents will pay at least $1,000 more per year under this plan.

Mayor Pugh acknowledged the concerns and recommended holding over the resolution. "I appreciate the comments made this evening and I am recommending that the Board hold-over the resolution," he said, adding that he would donate his savings back to the Village.

Trustee Slippen agreed, stating, "We have a responsibility for more communication and outreach and believes if more people knew what was going on, especially from those that will be paying more, they would have reached out."

**What's Next:**

*   The Board of Trustees will revisit the resolution at a future meeting.
*   The Village plans to conduct a vigorous outreach campaign to every resident who will be getting an increase.

# Croton-on-Hudson to Get New Dump Truck Thanks to State Grant

### A $100,000 grant from the Dormitory Authority of the State of New York (DASNY) will allow the Village to purchase a new dump truck for the Department of Public Works.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson has been awarded a $100,000 grant from the Dormitory Authority of the State of New York (DASNY) to purchase a new dump truck for the Department of Public Works (DPW). The funding comes from the Community Resiliency, Economic Sustainability, and Technology (CREST) Program.

The Board of Trustees unanimously approved Resolution #46-2025, authorizing Village Manager Bryan Healy to sign the Grant Disbursement Agreement with DASNY and execute any necessary documents for the grant's acceptance and administration.

The new dump truck will allow the DPW to continue providing essential services to the community.

# Croton to Advocate for Increased State Aid and Clean Water Funding

### The Village Board of Trustees is joining multi-municipality efforts to lobby for increased state aid and funding for clean water infrastructure projects.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is joining forces with other municipalities to advocate for increased state funding. The Board of Trustees approved two resolutions supporting these efforts at their March 12 meeting.

# Croton Joins Effort to Fight Con Edison Rate Hike

### The Village is joining other Westchester municipalities to hire special counsel and fight Con Edison's proposed electric and gas rate increases.

The Village of Croton-on-Hudson is joining a coalition of Westchester municipalities to fight a proposed Con Edison rate hike. The Board of Trustees approved Resolution #54-2025, authorizing the hiring of Joel R. Dichter of Dichter Law LLC as special counsel to represent the participating municipalities before the Public Service Commission.

Under Con Edison's petition, electric rates would rise by 18% with residential delivery charges increasing by 25.1%, and natural gas delivery rates rising by 19.1%. The proposal comes after a 20% increase in rates over the past three years.

The cost of representation will be split equally among all participating municipalities, with Croton's share not to exceed $100,000. The authorization is contingent on at least twenty municipalities agreeing to be represented by Dichter Law LLC.
