- Funding Sources/Issues
Len and Lindsay attended a 12/6 NYSERDA zoom on how it will handle IRA $ going to
the State for distribution. It discussed only residential incentives and did not say how it
will do so for non-residential muni sites. For residentials, it will take until “Q2 to Q4
2024”, i.e., another 6-12 months, to roll out the federal incentive programs, e.g., the
(up to) $1,750 for HPWHs.
On 12/13, NYSERDA announced an expanded CEC program. Some changes will
greatly increase our grant funding and/or give us more CEC points:
 new or higher value High Impact Actions (HIA); we’ve already done a few
 one free ASHRAE Level II or III energy audit (value: ~$10K-$15K+)
 points for work already done in the CSC program
 two new grants totaling $425,000 for CEC points we’ve already gathered.
To get the $425K, we must (within 3 months of NYSERDA formally notifying us of our
eligibility, probably in late February) identify and start work (e.g., designing) on
projects to use the money. Near term projects include solar and/or ASHPs at:
- Washington firehouse garage HPs (Jon said ~$50K + ~$15K for electrical work)*
- ~50 kW DPW canopy (~$200K) + ~$50K (Village cap. $?) to switch 4 A/Cs to HPs
- ~50 kW Harmon roof (~$190K + fix roof) & switch 6 A/Cs to HPs (Village cap. $?)
*maybe also convert oil boiler to gas and get rid of the 45-yr old underground oil tank
(maybe fund it from the $100K CREST money, which is not tied to NYSERDA rules)

his help on that task. We also got 600 points for installing a “renewable generation

Croton100 Climate Action Quilt
Croton100 is highlighting local climate progress with a Croton100 Climate Action Quilt (“CCAQ”)
(pronounced “c-sack”). This unique work of art threads together depictions of decarbonizing actions by
Croton households, businesses and public organizations in zip code 10520.
The Quilt will translate adoption of climate crisis solutions into a tangible visualization of our communitywide action. It will grow over time and serve as a vivid depiction of momentum in our progress towards
achieving net zero carbon emissions.
CCAQ has three patch sizes, corresponding to:
- Small Hitter actions (edible gardens, composting, electric stoves, raising chickens, building backup
battery, hot water heaters, pescatarian diet, plug-in hybrid vehicle. Small hitter actions do not include
building energy efficiency measures like energy efficient windows and insulation, air clothes drying.
- Big Hitter actions (heat pumps, EVs, clean electricity and significant contributions to the green economy),
- Impact Multiplier actions like many projects the Village has completed, and public facilities like heat
pumps and solar panels on Houses of Worship, etc.
Volunteers are needed for many aspects of this project. For more information and to volunteer, contact:
Communications@croton100.org.
