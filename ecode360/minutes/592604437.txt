3. QUESTIONS/COMMENTS SUBMITTED BY E-MAIL
Colleen Beyrer, 19 Wells Avenue, Croton on Hudson, asked why the scope
of the new multi-residential development at 41-51 Maple Street changed
to now include eleven, three bedroom apartments, she is concerned that
these changed were pushed through at the eleventh hour.
Mayor Pugh stated that the change in the distribution of the bedrooms is
the result of further negotiations between the Board and the Applicant,
two weeks ago we approved a “Term Sheet” but it is not a binding
contract and in the course of our discussions, the Applicant provided
documentation to the effect that it would be a stronger application for
funding if it included three bedroom apartments and the Board felt that
this was an equitable and reasonable distribution for a diversity of housing
options. Mayor Pugh stated that the Board also communicated to the
Applicant that we expected a higher “PILOT” as a result because three
bedroom apartments can collect a higher rent and they were amenable to
that and the details of the final agreement are still being discussed.

Resolution #27 of 2021
On motion of TRUSTEE SIMON, seconded by TRUSTEE HABIB, the following
resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson,
New York with the following Roll Call Vote: Trustee Habib, Aye, Trustee Simon Aye,
Trustee Gallelli Aye, Trustee Horowitz Aye, Mayor Pugh Aye.
WHEREAS, on June 12, 2020, Governor Andrew Cuomo signed Executive Order 203,
requiring local municipalities with police departments to develop a reform plan
based on community input; and
WHEREAS on September 25, 2020, the Village issued a call for volunteers to serve
on a task force for the purposes of developing the aforementioned plan; and
WHEREAS the task force held a number of public forums for residents to provide
input on the police department and conducted a survey on the community’s
interactions with the police department, which was completed by over 300
residents, and
WHEREAS the draft police reform plan that was developed has taken into account
all of the above feedback; and
WHEREAS the Village Board of Trustees held a public hearing on February 22, 2021
to hear public comments on the Police Reform Task Force Plan; and
WHEREAS following the public hearing, the Village Board reviewed the action items
proposed by the plan; and
5

WHEREAS the plan has been revised to now include 15 priorities which the Village
Board supports,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED: that the Village Board of Trustees hereby adopts
the Village of Croton-on-Hudson Police Reform Task Force Plan; and
AND BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED: that certification of such plan (“Appendix B”) shall
be transmitted to the state budget director as required by Executive Order 203.
DISCUSSION:
Trustee Simon stated that we are a better Croton for having gone through this
process and believes it is responsive to the outline that the Governor has provided
for this process and that the recommendations are responsive and consistent with
what was being discussed in the many meetings we held. Trustee Simon stated
that a report like this has got to be more of a blueprint then a last word and one of
the most important things in our recommendations is our commitment to establish
a “Police Advisory Board”; that body will help guide our community in the ways in
which this report is implemented as well as come up with additional ideas and
suggestions for how we can improve policing in the future. Trustee Simon stated
that the recommendations are filled with substance and will help guide us into the
future and thanked the Task Force, Police Chief and Manager for all their hard work.
Trustee Horowitz stated that she agrees with Ms. Lukin’s evaluation of the
language, this is an important milestone for us and an opportunity for us to take a
really close look at what we were mandated to do, through a long and thorough
process we came up with fifteen recommendations that we are committed to but
she does not feel that the word “endeavor” is strong enough and the language
should reflect our commitment to this process. Trustee Horowitz stated that she
hopes that the “Police Advisory Council” will be a strong part of this plan and a
means to be a continuous interaction between the Police Department and members
of that Committee.
Trustee Gallelli stated that she agrees with substantially everything she heard
tonight, moving through this process and now the culmination of this report and the
plan will make Croton a better Village and believes that we are all in support of this.
Trustee Gallelli stated that our “Task Force” members took this very seriously and
thanked them for their participation; as we move ahead, she believes that many of
these things will actually develop and looks forward to a “Police Advisory Council”,

which will be made up of members of our community, participating and providing
regular feedback to the Board.
Trustee Habib stated that this is an historic time for the Village, we are shaking off
decades of dis-engagement with the Police Department, we have never gone
through an a-z review of the Police Department nor have we been able to act upon
certain reforms and thanked everyone for undertaking this difficult exercise and if
we look at this document as a wonderful blueprint for the future, we might also
want to look at it as a report card.
Village Attorney Whitehead clarified that “Section 35.30 of the Penal Law” allows
the use of physical force under certain circumstances and even deadly physical
force under other circumstances. Village Attorney Whitehead stated that Croton’s
policy is actually more protective then the “Penal Law” because it talks about the
officers having to use the minimal force necessary. Village Attorney Whitehead
stated that the Law never intended to say that “chokeholds” could not be used,
there are instances where they can be deemed to be justified and be an affirmative
offense; the Croton Police force has never used “chokeholds” and with the language
about using the minimal force necessary she would not anticipate that anyone on
our Police force would use a “chokehold”.
Mayor Pugh asked Chief Nikitopoulos if he can briefly discuss some of the updates
to the “Standard Operating Procedures” (“SOP’s”) over the last few months and
some of the other areas he will be looking at.
Chief Nikitopoulos stated that we have been working on our Policies for quite some
time, but because of “Police Reform” we pushed to get it done sooner and to date
eighty percent of the Policies have been put out to the Department and we expect
the rest to be completed by mid-March; some of the changes include removing
some of the standards with respect to issuing tickets, revising how we handle
complaints and requiring Supervisors to write evaluations completely and honestly
so that they can be used as a training tool and be addressed properly. Chief
Nikitopoulos stated that he reached out to the “Westchester County Department of
Community Mental Health” and we have contact information that we are going to
start to use, there are mental health assessment services that are available to us as
well, we are also connected to several other organizations and while they will not
respond to a scene, if a Police Officer feels they need more assistance we have
people we can contact.

WHEREAS, the Village of Croton-on-Hudson has owned the parcel located at 41-51
Maple Street (“the Property”) since 2008; and
WHEREAS, the Village Board of Trustees has determined the Property is not needed
for municipal purposes; and
WHEREA, the Property is located in a C-2 Zoning District and within the Municipal
Place Gateway Overlay District, allowing for a variety of commercial and multifamily residential uses; and
WHEREAS on February 17, 2021 the Village Board adopted Resolution 25-2021 to
move forward with Regan Development Corporation (“the Applicant”) of Ardsley,
New York, as the proposed purchaser and developer of the Property; and
WHEREAS the Village Board is in receipt of a special permit application for a multiunit residential building and the requisite $3,000 application fee from the Applicant,
and

WHEREAS the special permit application stipulates that the project will now consist
of 11 one-bedroom, 11 two-bedroom and 11 three-bedroom units; and
WHEREAS the Applicant has submitted Part 1 of an Environmental Assessment Form
and a Coastal Assessment Form,
NOW, THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED: that the Village Board hereby determines that
the Proposed Action is an unlisted action under SEQRA; and
BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED: that the Village Board hereby declares its intent to be
the Lead Agency for SEQRA purposes in connection with the proposed action and

application by the various Boards.
DISCUSSION:
Village Attorney Whitehead explained that this is only the beginning of the process,
they will need a “Special Permit” to allow for the proposed residential use under the
Zoning as well as “Site Plan Approval”; the Village Board, as “Lead Agency”, will
perform the “SEQRA Review and a “Consistency Review” under the “LWRP”,
following that review, the Board will then schedule a Public Hearing on the “Special
Permit” and once those reviews have been completed and if the Board grants the
“Special Permit”, it will then go back to the Planning Board for a more detailed
“Site Plan” review.
Mayor Pugh stated that although the Board approved the “Term Sheet”, it is not
binding, and we continue to bargain with the applicant on the final details.
