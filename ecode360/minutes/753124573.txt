resolution was adopted by the Board of Trustees of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson, New

WHEREAS the Village of Croton-on-Hudson seeks to amend the Code of the Village of
Croton-on-Hudson by adding a new Chapter 186 entitled "Rental Housing" to implement
the provisions of the Good Cause Eviction Law enacted by the State of New York, and
WHEREAS Local Law Introductory No. 15 of 2024 was drafted for such purposes, and

Village Board of Trustees

Page 6

WHEREAS a public hearing was opened and closed on January 8, 2025,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees hereby adopts
Local Law Introductory No. 15 of 2024, adding a new Chapter 186 entitled "Rental
Housing" to the Code of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson to implement the provisions of
the Good Cause Eviction Law enacted by the State of New York, which upon adoption
becomes Local Law No. 1 of 2025.
DISCUSSION
Trustee Politi stated that meeting notices are on our website with the hope that
interested parties will attend. Trustee Politi stated that not only does this legislation
protect tenants, it also addresses the increased costs that landlords are experiencing and
will allow landlords to increase rents to reflect those increases so that they can continue
to make improvements on their properties, but giving tenants the ability to log a
complaint with a landlord that their heat is not working and not to be afraid of being
kicked out of their apartment is a good thing.
Trustee Simon stated that the Good Cause Eviction legislation was enacted by the
Legislature and emphasized that neither side got everything they wanted, they listened
to all sides, and after the legislation was passed in Albany the Board held two work
sessions and discussed all the issues. Trustee Simon stated that this legislation is an
additional avenue to resolve landlord/tenant issues, we also received about 250 letters
from the Real Estate Industry, but we believe that the fears that were expressed will not
be realized. Trustee Simon stated that the FAQ (Frequently Asked Questions) provides
information and suggested everyone take a look at that information. Trustee Simon
stated that 40% of renters in Croton are rent burdened, which means that 1/3 of their
income goes to rent, Westchester already has one of the highest rents in the State,
inflation plus 5% will be the base for those covered under this law and it seems fair to
him, he does not want renters in Croton to be second class citizens when it comes to the
protections that they deserve, and this legislation will give tenants and landlords the
tools to resolve their issues.
Trustee Nicholson stated that when the Board passed the ETPA legislation, we had an
opportunity for our landlords to ask questions and she encourages the Board to do the
same with respect to this new legislation. Trustee Nicholson stated that in 2021 our
Housing Task Force asked us to do a rental registry, and hopes that we can get this done
in 2025, because it is important for us to be able to communicate to our tenants and
landlords, of the 1,500 rental units in Croton we are only aware of 300 of those renters
and as we are taking on new laws and responsibilities we owe it to our community to
communicate in the most effective way.

Village Board of Trustees
January 8, 2025
Page 7

Village Manager Healy advised that the 300 units signifies apartments that are three
family or more because those buildings are subject to more stringent inspections, a lot of
those other units are either accessory apartments or two-family homes which do not
have the same inspection requirements but we do have access to who owns those
properties and we can certainly include those properties in the registry.
Trustee Slippen stated that while she was not present during those work sessions, she
did have the opportunity go back and listen to those work sessions, and even though we
are committed to different channels to communicate, she feels that we missed the mark
in terms of making sure that both tenants and landlords were able to participate in this
conversation and would have liked to have heard from more renters and landlords to get
a more complete picture exactly how everyone is feeling about this. Trustee Slippen
stated that in tight housing markets with low vacancy rates tenant protections may be
more impactful since renters have fewer alternatives, and in markets with more housing
availability the potential market distortion effects becomes less concerning, in cities with
Good Cause Eviction laws they have found eviction rates decrease and housing stability
increased particularly for vulnerable populations, but research has indicated potential
negative effects on housing supply and property investments as well. Trustee Slippen
stated that Croton is in a development phase, and we are in the process of adding more
opportunities for both tenants and landlords and believes it is a good opportunity for
Croton at this time.
Trustee Nicholson stated that this law will force more cases into the Courts that are
already overburdened and underfunded in the State, in Croton we are fortunate that we
have a Village Court and when we look at the data of the number of eviction cases we
only see about two a month but she would like to see the State expand their funding and
support for local municipalities who adopt this legislation.
Mayor Pugh stated that the Board also approved Rent Stabilization Guidelines as well as
an Emergency Tenant Protection Act and believes that the Good Cause Eviction law is a
common-sense measure that will give tenants the security that many of us take for
granted. Mayor Pugh stated that the American Bar Association lists ten guidelines for
eviction laws, and guideline #9 states that “lease termination including non-renewal should be
limited to circumstances where good cause exists” and believes we are adopting what is
recognized as a best practice which has existed in other states for decades and has finally
come to New York State.

F.O.I.L. Resolution:
On motion by Trustee Slippen and seconded by Trustee Simon, the Board of
Trustees of the Village of Croton-on-Hudson approved the following response to a
Freedom of Information request from Mr. Michael Balter. Motion was approved with
a 5-0 vote.
This is to inform you that on January 8, 2025, the Croton-on-Hudson Village Board of
Trustees (“Village Board”) met as the FOIL Appeals Board to consider the Village’s
December 20, 2024, response to your December 12, 2024, FOIL request.
Your said FOIL sought records of “all communications, no matter what their form (emails,
text messages, notes of meetings or telephone or Zoom calls, recordings, photographs,
and all other documents) between developer Phil Spagnoli and Village Engineer Dan
O’Connor, that concern, reference, mention, or otherwise refer to the village owned
property at 60 Whelan Avenue.”
Your request for records was responded to on the basis that no such records existed or
could be located. Please be advised the FOIL Appeals Board hereby upholds that
determination. Herein the Village’s FOIL Officer personally met with relevant staff,
