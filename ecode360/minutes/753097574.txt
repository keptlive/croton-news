4. ADJOURNMENT:

There being no further business, Leo Wiegman made a motion for adjournment, seconded by lan
Murtaugh, and the meeting adjourned at 4:57 p.m.

Ed Riely of 110 Truesdale Drive was present and spoke saying said that anything in parking lot A has an
effect on the shore. Ed Riely also noted that Croton Point Park is accessed by one road, given that it is
unlikely that people will come through Half Moon Bay and a development at this location, of this size will
present problems. Mr. Riely said that if the proposed development were three-stories high, it would be
acceptable. Mr. Riely does not believe that the proposed building fits in architecturally or environmentally.
Mr. Riely

Tel: 914-271-4783 www.crotononhudson-ny.gov Fax: 914-271-3790


also has concerns that the building, as proposed, will affect the views from several blocks in the Harmon
area, including Wayne Street, Young Avenue and Benedict Boulevard. Lastly, Mr. Riely noted that he
believes that there are problems with the sewer lines already, and that the pumps break down, and this
new development would make it worse.

Michael Mamone of 19 Young Avenue also believes that the proposed development at 1-3 Croton Point
Avenue is way too big, and agrees with all the issues raised by Mr. Riely. Michael Mamone questions the
104 proposed parking spots proposed for 100 new dwelling units, believing that the development will bring
many more cars, and “Where will they park”? Michael Mamone says that there is already overload of
parking from South Riverside Avenue businesses on Young Avenue and other streets.

Michael Mamone also expressed concern about the proposal at 325 South Riverside Avenue, as,
according to Mr. Mamone, the intersection near the new building is already dangerous with most cars
going above the speed limit. Mr. Mamone suggests a safety investigation of the intersection (of Oneida
Avenue and South Riverside Avenue).

Gary Eisinger of 210 Cleveland drive spoke next regarding the 1-3 Croton Point Avenue application. Gary
Eisinger was disappointed that the Waterfront Advisory Committee did not look at how the overlarge
building will look from the water, when out on a boat, and this is an important piece that is missing from the
presentations. Gary Eisinger believes that the proposal at 1-3 Croton Point Avenue is way out of scale,
and will impact the viewshed from Route 9 and many residents of the Village.

Stuart Greenbaum reiterated that the purview of the Waterfront Advisory Committee is to make a
consistency review of a proposed 100-unit residential building located at 1-3 Croton Point Avenue (and all
other applications). The WAC review does not constitute an approval of the proposal, however.

The WAC meeting of September 26, 2024 concluded at 5:39 p.m.

DATE: September 30, 2024

On Thursday, September 26, 2024, the Waterfront Advisory Committee (WAC) reviewed for
consistency with the Village’s Local Waterfront Revitalization Program (LWRP) the referral listed
above. Based on the Committee’s review of the 44 LWRP policies, the Waterfront Advisory
Committee made a recommendation of consistency with the LWRP.

The WAC reviewed the Coastal Assessment Form that was completed by the applicant and had
the following changes:

1) In section C, part 1, the entire Village is in the coastal area.

2) Section C number 4 “o,” should be marked “Yes” as the proposed development will alter, and
improve water runoff from the site after it passes through the treatment units.

3) Section D, the WAC would add “The Village has enough domestic water to supply the project.
Aconnection to the Westchester County sewer force main is proposed for sewage disposal.
The current building uses were not factored into assessing the water and sewage demand.

The committee reviewed the 44 LWRP policies and determined that the following policies are
relevant:

POLICY 1A: Encourage integrated development of Village property to assure fulfillment
of requirements relating to parking and accessory uses of Metro-North train station,
while facilitating public access to the bay area and recreational use.

POLICY 5: Encourage the location of development in areas where public services and
facilities essential to such development are adequate.

POLICY 5A: When feasible, development within the current service area of existing water
and sewer facilities or in close proximity to areas where distribution lines currently exist.

POLICY 6: Expedite permit procedures in order to facilitate the siting of development
activities at suitable locations.

POLICY 11: Buildings and other structures will be sited in the coastal area so as to
minimize damage to property and the endangering of human lives caused by flooding
and erosion.

POLICY 12: Activities or development in the coastal area will be undertaken so as to
minimize damage to natural resources and property from flooding and erosion by
protecting natural protective features including beaches, dunes, barrier islands and
bluffs.

POLICY 18: To safeguard the vital economic, social, and environmental interests of the
State and of its citizens, proposed major actions in the coastal area must give full
consideration to those interests, and to the safeguards which the State has established
to protect valuable coastal resource areas.

POLICY 33: Best Management Practices will be used to ensure the control of stormwater
runoff and combined sewer overflows draining into coastal waters.

POLICY 33A: The flow of stormwater discharge shall be controlled to limit the flow of
pollutants from street and parking areas, etc. directly into the rivers and water bodies.

POLICY 41: Land use or development in the coastal area will not cause national or State
air quality standards to be violated.

The proposed project is close to the Croton-Harmon train station and on a site that can be
served by public water and sewer connections.

The project is not in the floodplain and is a redevelopment of a site with a building and parking
lots.

Storm water will be treated prior to discharge into existing storm water systems

It is for the reasons contained in this memorandum that the WAC issues its recommendation of
consistency.

Consistency review for NY State Department of Transportation federal funding for
cleaning/washing bridges and sealing decks on U.S. Route 9 bridges over New York

State Route 129.
DATE: September 30, 2024

On Thursday, September 26, 2024, the Waterfront Advisory Committee (WAC) reviewed for
consistency with the Village’s Local Waterfront Revitalization Program (LWRP) the referral listed
above. Based on the Committee’s review of the 44 LWRP policies, the Waterfront Advisory
Committee made a recommendation of consistency with the LWRP.

The WAC reviewed the Coastal Assessment Form and had no changes.

The committee reviewed the 44 LWRP policies and determined that the following policies are
relevant:

POLICY 18: To safeguard the vital economic, social, and environmental interests of the
State and of its citizens, proposed major actions in the coastal area must give full
consideration to those interests, and to the safeguards which the State has established
to protect.

POLICY 30: Municipal, industrial, and commercial discharge of pollutants, including but
not limited to toxic and hazardous substances, into coastal waters will conform to State
and National water quality standards.

POLICY 33: Best Management Practices will be used to ensure the control of stormwater
runoff and combined sewer overflows draining into coastal waters.

POLICY 39: The transport, storage, treatment and disposal of solid wastes, particularly
hazardous wastes, within coastal areas will be conducted in such a manner so as to
protect groundwater and surface water supplies, significant fish and wildlife habitats,
recreation areas, important agricultural lands and scenic resources.

The specifications for the work show that the work will be performed in a manner that is
protective of the environment. The cleaning and sealing work will extend the life of the
infrastructure.

It is for the reasons contained in this memorandum that the WAC issues its recommendation of
consistency.
