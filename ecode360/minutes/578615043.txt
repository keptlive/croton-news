Announcements:
On Dec. 7, Mayor Pugh will announce the addition of two new members to our committee.
- Dan Columbini, PE, is also a member of the school’s Environmental Sustainability Committee
(ESC). Dan is a mechanical engineer at Goldman Copeland Associates, a New York City
engineering consulting firm. He heads its department on plumbing and fire safety. He is building a
passive solar house for his family in Teatown, to be equipped with air source heat pumps, solar
panels, and a HPWH, to be completed in 2022.
- Bernie Yozwiak is known to many of us for his work with the Care for Creation (C4C) Ministry at
Croton’s Holy Name Church. He is a senior electrical engineer with WSP, a general engineering
services firm. He was previously a VP with Cosentini Associates, an engineering firm based in New
York City. Via the C4C, Bernie and his associates have provided educational events related to
climate change, heat pumps, EVs, and other issues relevant to our work.
We welcome both to our committee, and look forward to their contributions.
Quick updates/holdovers/follow-ups/status:
- Solar projects:
- DPW rooftop: the system is now outputting power. Rent to the Village should start next month.
- Croton Landing canopy: In November’s election, State Senator Peter Harckham was re-elected.
Harckham originated a bill which, if passed, would help overcome the Croton Landing siting issue.
Senators May, Gaughran, and Metzger are co-sponsors, all of whom were re-elected. To move that
bill forward (it already passed the State Senate), we are seeking a companion bill and sponsors in
the Assembly, via Sandy Galef. Brian indicated he would follow up to urge her participation.
[Lindsay asked Sustainable Westchester how it could help.]
- Train station parking lot canopy: On Nov. 11, all 7 bidders provided revised proposals that
complied with our requested changes, e.g., all now use 22% for their Investment Tax Credit (ITC).
Doing so reduced their lease payment offerings, providing more realistic numbers for our review.
Janine is reviewing SolSystems draft lease as the front runner. [As of 12/11, that effort has been
expedited with a zoom scheduled for 12/15 to work out lease agreement issues.]
1

Two financial issues may impact the value of the train station project and possibly other smaller
efforts. Next month, ITC drops from 26% to 22%, reducing annual lease rent by $50K-$100K,
depending on installed kW. We look forward to the Biden administration extending the ITC for five
years, as proposed by renewables advocates. On Nov. 24, Con Ed announced that it had reached
its required MW level for community solar (CS) in Westchester County. No more CS projects will
get the CS $.10/kWh VDER community credit unless PSC expands the program. Ecogy has
stopped pitching CS here.
Brian suggested that Houses of Worship (HoW) be convened to discuss how their properties (esp.
parking areas) could be considered for solar installations (e.g., canopies). Bernie has a contact list
for 7 of them that we may use, previously developed through a Croton100 effort to promote heat
pumps to HoWs. The Interfaith Council that previously acted as a clearinghouse for such activities
is dormant, so the Village could instead host the meeting, preferably when face-to-face events are
again available in the late spring or early summer of 2021.
- A non-municipal 226-kW CS project opened Nov. 13 atop a self-storage facility in Tarrytown,
installed by Sunrise Solar Solutions. Ecogy’s Arcadia Farm CS system in Yorktown is nearing
completion, with power to start flowing in the next month or two.
- EV/EVSE:
- The newly formed school bus electrification task force (BETF) zoomed on Nov. 19. It laid out the
decision process and created subgroups to focus on aspects of the issue. Based on 2018 energy
data, the bus fleet accounts for 17.4% (372 tons/yr) of the school district’s GHG emissions (almost
as much as one of the school buildings). Many thoughts and ideas were offered. Rochelle O’Mara
announced that the district bought its first plug-in hybrid (a Prius Prime) for internal mail delivery
after a tree fell on the previously used vehicle. BETF next meets on Dec. 21, during which Lindsay
will offer a proposal that involves a solar canopy project at the bus depot. It could help bankroll both
the necessary EV infrastructure and electric buses.
- At a Nov. 19 Con Ed zoom on its Make Ready EV power infrastructure program, it discussed the
Make Ready program as it would cover non-public EV sites serving light-duty vehicles. During a
private zoom on Dec. 3, Con Ed rep Alison Kling reviewed a parallel tariff-based incentive for
heavy-duty vehicles that could offer more $ toward interconnection and distribution costs. [She later
emailed that the program could cover 87.5% of those costs at the bus depot.]
- Frank Balbi agreed to provide data that will help us determine at what truck voucher funding level
(and in what year) it may be feasible to replace a diesel garbage truck with a fully electric unit. The
increased level of truck (but not school bus) vouchers will help clarify that option. We discussed the
possibility of replacing an existing Village pickup truck with an electric Ford F150s when they
appear in 2021/2022. However, Croton has only F250 to F550 pickup trucks. Ford has not
announced any plans to produce electric versions of those heftier trucks. Lindsay will ask Frank if a
F150 could do the job of one of them. Brian pointed out, however, that the Village’s financial
condition presently bars such an expenditure, even if a F150 could do so.
- Food scrap recycling: We may have a project coordinator. Croton resident Rob Abbot (retired
management consultant recently moved to Croton) is interested in helping. After a Nov. 30 chat
with Lindsay, Rob will get back to us regarding what tasks he may be willing to handle. Joel and
Lindsay continue working with Donna O’Malley on the survey, flyer, and web posting. On Nov. 23,

- We await details on NYSERDA’s new CEC grant program (called its “Leadership Round”) and

- Jon Katz volunteered his son (Steven) to do a web search for others’ HPWH flyers and web

community service requirement. Lindsay will follow up with Jon/Steven to do so.
- Jon Katz analyzed potential savings for homes having gas-fired DHW heaters, both with and
without solar panels. He concluded that there would be no $ savings for such homes lacking PV.
But those having PV panels (there are 180+ in Croton) could save if their systems outputted at
least 1,500 excess kWh/yr to run the HPWH. Bernie made a similar point regarding homes with
solar that have oil-fired systems (such as his). We will address both in our HPWH promotions.

Communications:
- NYPA’s renewable power offer: Despite a zoom and follow-up emails, NYPA’s rep says it needs
more time to provide the numbers needed to determine the differential cost for power that would be
bundled with hydro RECs. That package could enable the Village and/or school district to state that
their electricity was considered renewable.
- After much research, we learned that the power use at the Croton-Harmon train maintenance yard
(Croton’s largest carbon emitter) is not metered. Instead, it is mixed with the traction power for the
Hudson Line trains, making it impossible to fully analyze the buildings’ usage. The impacts of that
deficiency were briefly discussed, with an eye to pressing MTA/State energy officials to add a
traction sub-meter to correct it.

I looked at the Rocky Mountain Institute and the World Resources Institute for programs potentially useful to
the Sustainability Committee and found interesting results. I also clarified how Regional Greenhouse Gas
Initiative funds are used. Here's a quick summary for your review.
The Rocky Mountain Institute (RMI). RMI notes that, "In the US, cities and states are at the forefront of
climate change risk and opportunity. And while many are making climate commitments, they need strategic,
cost-effective solutions and the technical assistance to bring those intentions to reality". Of particular interest
to the Committee could be the RMI Carbon Free Cities and States program which includes four main initiatives:
(1) America's Pledge; (2) Urban Mobility Lab; (3) Residential Energy+; and City Renewables Accelerator.
RMI says, "... we can deliver a holistic suite of services to cities, including cross-cutting research and analysis,
facilitation of diverse stakeholders, and access to industry experts throughout our vast network. Our strategy
is to work with cohorts of cities and focus on a limited number of near-term, high impact activities that can be
4

launched quickly with concrete results". I have written to RMI staff with background on the Committee and
asked how we could work with them.
World Resources Institute (WRI). The Ross Center for Sustainable Cities is the WRI program "dedicated
to shaping a future where cities work better for everyone". Relevant programs include Buildings, Sustainable
Urban Mobility, Urban Climate Resilience, Water, Energy and Climate, and Urban Development.
Much of their focus is international, but I wrote to their Director with background on the Committee and asked
how we could work with them. I received a positive response, which I'll review tomorrow.
The NY Regional Greenhouse Gas Initiative (RGGI). Following up on your RGGI questions, I reviewed
the material you sent, the RGGI website and operating plan. It appears that RGGI auction proceeds flow into
NYSERDA, which then uses those resources for numerous CO2 emission reduction programs. NYSERDA
notes it "designed and implemented a process to develop and annually update an Operating Plan which
summarizes and describes the initiatives to be supported by RGGI auction proceeds. The RGGI Operating
Plan is designed to strategically invest across disciplines, economy wide, in a way that supports comprehensive
strategies that best advance the [goals] of the State".
I hope the above is all helpful and look forward to our meeting tomorrow morning._______________________________________________________________________________________
