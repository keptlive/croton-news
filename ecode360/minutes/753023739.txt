- Solar Projects/Issues
Train station canopies: completion is still scheduled for Oct. 2024.
DPW solar parking canopies: The concept involved a ~50 kW unit to be financed and
owned by the Village and a ~150 kW unit to be financed and owned by a developer,
e.g., Ecogy. The ~50 kW system would provide all power (via net metering) needed by
the DPW building. The ~150 kW system would be community solar providing solar
billing credits to 9 municipal accounts. [Bryan Healy was also interested in enrolling
other Village accounts to other community solar facilities.] Together, the canopies
could save the Village $5K to $9K/yr on its electric bills while cutting ~94 MTCO2e/yr,
about 23 of which would be counted against our muni carbon footprint.
However, on 2/20, Ecogy passed on the option, saying it was too small to work
financially. Rising interest rates and construction costs, lower incentives, and falling
value of transferable tax credits (due to a growing glut of them) are to blame. Only
canopies at least 400-500 kW now make economic sense to developers.
We discussed several DPW options, including the Village bankrolling the ~150 kW
canopy and remotely net metering its output to Village accounts, and fully electrifying
DPW using large HPs to eliminate use of oil. Jon and Lindsay had looked at a HP for
the garage part of DPW several years ago, but found it too costly. Lindsay will review
with Jon and Dan the above options for discussion at our March meeting.
Harmon firehouse rooftop option: the proposed ~50 kW rooftop and A/C-to-HP
conversion is being developed for integration with the planned 2,500 SF addition to
the building. See further discussion under “heat pumps”.
Brian and Len told us of plans for the Gouveia Park house which (in 2025) will become
home to the Recreation Dept. A solar array on its rooftop and/or a ground-mounted
system were discussed, with the latter pondered as a source of remote net-metered
power to other park-related accounts. [Lindsay subsequently found that a ~72 kW
array could supply all Village park electric accounts, though converting the Gouveia
Park house to full use and heat pumps might bump that to ~80 kW. The Gouveia
House has a ~2,000 SF flat roof able to produce ~20 kWp, perhaps enough to run the
building, but only after cutting down many trees presently shading the roof.]
Brian described a recent Lewisboro legal opinion that the State alienation law may
allow such use of park land if the power went only to other municipal parks. [Brian
agreed to send it to Lindsay for review.]

Regarding the 30% ITC tax credit for EV chargers, our research found that incentive is
limited to federal census tracts considered non-urban and/or low-income. [Brian
subsequently confirmed that finding.] That excludes the Croton train station (where we
plan to install 24 EV charge ports), along with many Metro-North train station parking
lots in our County. The credit would have provided us with ~$45K to help cover extra
costs due to inflation, etc. after we developed our cost estimate in July 2022.
This rule also applies to anyone wishing to install a charger at their home if it’s within
such a tract, e,g., all of Croton. [Details and a map were sent to committee members.].
- Funding Sources/Issues
For the Washington firehouse garage HPs, Jon is getting proposals and expects a
total cost (HP + electrical work) to be about $65K. If so, we could pay for the work
using most of the $82.5K in grants from recent Community Campaigns.
On 2/22, NYSERDA formally notified us that, as a result of our accumulation of 9,000

For the Washington firehouse, we discussed dealing with its 45-yr old underground oil

because existing HPs are unable to meet the load on very cold days. Brian said that
the fungible CREST grant money could be used for an electric boiler but not for
anything that continued use of fossil fuels. NYSERDA grants can’t be used for either

- New Business / Other Issues
Len and Brian discussed Croton’s new status as a Pro Housing Community. On
2/7/2024, the Village of Croton-on-Hudson was officially designated by Governor
Kathy Hochul as one of the first 20 Pro-Housing Communities in New York
State. Croton received the designation after documenting that our housing stock has
increased by 1% in the previous year or 3% over the previous 3 years. Deputy Mayor
Len Simon joined fellow officials in Albany for a roundtable discussion on the program
and the future of housing in New York State. This designation gives the Village priority
consideration for up to $650 million in state discretionary funding, including these
programs: the Downtown Revitalization Initiative, NY Forward, Regional Council
Capital Fund, NY Main Street, Market NY Capital Grants, Mid-Hudson Momentum
Fund, and Public Transportation Modernization Enhancement Program.
