BoT Referral - Local Law 7 of 2025
● See letter from Village Clerk below
● Consider select roadway restrictions for electric assist bikes and scooters
● “…consider prohibiting the use of bicycles with electric assist and electric
scooters in certain Village -owned public lands and properties for a
comprehensive review and comments back to the Board on the law, including
safety and signage”
● Committee position / recommendations:
● Committee does not deem necessary any general or specific restrictions on ebike usage on already permissible roads or pathways at this time. E-bikes and
e-scooters should adhere to the same guidelines and regulations that apply to
bicycles as currently codified.
● As a safety management tool, the Committee would recommend posting 10 mph
speed limit signage applying to cyclists along the shared pathway next to Elliott
Way (that stretches from Half Moon Bay to Senasqua park), as well as at the
entrance of Croton Landing.
● Note that all bikes (pedal assist or otherwise) should continue to use the
shared roadway on Elliott Way between Senasqua and the Yaght club as
currently dictated.
● The Committee will be continue to monitor incidents regarding e-bike usage
around the village, observant of issues raised, willing to update
recommendations if warranted.
● Other discussion notes:
● Section of pathway from HMB to Sensaqua – best practice would be to use the
road vs the shared pathway, but not be restricted.
● No need for specific restrictions for e-bikes; to be treated just as any bike (as
defined by NYS law)
● Speed limit posting - to be treated as a safety management tool - but not
necessarily codified
● Action: Matt to submit letter to Village Manager and BoT with BPC recommendations

Bike “licenses” for children (suggestion submitted by prior member)
● Response: setting aside the legality of such a system, or the local infrastructure
to administer or enforce it, general consensus is that we would not endorse a
system that discouraged or could in any way be punititive to young riders
prompted by the actions of a few bad actors. That said, we strongly endorse
further educating our young riders on safe riding and behaviors – and would
encourage the CPD to address these problematic (and dangerous) riders as
appropriate.
