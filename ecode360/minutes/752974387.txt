Resolution 202 of 2023
On motion of TRUSTEE HOROWITZ, seconded by TRUSTEE the following resolution was
put forth for adoption:
WHEREAS the Village Board is committed to promoting openness, inclusion, freedom and
mutual understanding by ensuring all people, regardless of race, religion, disability,
sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin or immigration status may live free
from violence, harassment or prejudice, and
WHEREAS the Village Board is further committed to supporting the visibility, dignity, and
equality for all people in the community,
NOW THEREFORE BE IT RESOLVED that the Statement of Inclusivity of September 26,
2023, is approved and adopted in order to foster and advance the goals of making the
Village of Croton-on-Hudson a just, equitable, diverse and inclusive community.
Statement of Inclusivity
September 26, 2023
The Village Board is committed to promoting openness, inclusion, freedom and mutual
understanding by ensuring all people, regardless of national origin, immigration status,
race, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, disability, or political viewpoint
may live free from violence, harassment or prejudice. We as a Village Board condemn
any acts of unlawful discrimination, violence and intimidation that target differences in
national origin, race, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, religion, disability,
immigration status, or political viewpoint across our nation. Such acts run counter to the
rights and freedoms upon which our country was founded and to the core values of the
Village of Croton-on-Hudson. The Village Board is committed to fostering a just,
equitable, diverse and inclusive community.
DISCUSSION:
Mayor Pugh thanked those who brought attention to the existing deficiencies in our
current statement with respect to the absence of language regarding sexual orientation
and gender identity, and while this was inadvertently left out of the original statement, it
has been recognized elsewhere in our original founding resolution as well as in our June
Pride Month resolution. Mayor Pugh added that he understands the desire for more
extensive dialogue, but he also believes that it was important that we address this

Village Board of Trustees

deficiency now and if the Diversity and Inclusion Committee wants to provide further
thoughts, they will be welcomed by the Board.
Trustee Simon stated that he not only views this as an update but a point of departure
and applauds everyone who made specific suggestions. Trustee Simon stated that he
looks forward to working on the concerns raised this evening, this is the first of many
steps and looks forward to hearing comments and recommendations from our Diversity
and Inclusion Committee as well as our community.
Trustee Nicholson stated that in spirit she supports this, but we should set a time to meet
with the community to get a consensus of what they feel is important, she does not
believe that we are really embracing the words if we do it in such short order and
suggested that we talk about it a bit further before we pass this resolution.
Trustee Horowitz stated that she believes there is a bit of a difference on the Board about
when and how the process works and believes that this statement belongs on the
Village’s web page in a very prominent place and would like the Board to table the
resolution so that we have a chance to discuss it further. Trustee Horowitz made the
following statement: “after the first protest occurred in response to the Drag Queen story hour
by The Dam Book Store, she and the Mayor had a conversation because she was concerned that
Village residents did not know how to best respond to such overt harassment about LBGTQ +
friends and neighbors and in response to that conversation the Mayor suggested that she create a
core of interested and concerned individuals to seek direction and guidance for a community-wide
exploration towards a constructive response. In response to that suggestion, she reached out to
the Croton Clergy, past and present Diversity and Inclusion Committee members, impacted
community members, PRIDE Organizers and concerned individuals involved in equity work in our
local schools, as a group we have been meeting weekly over the past couple of months, and have
been engaged in a very deliberate process of articulating a Village statement that supports,
protects and affirms all Croton on Hudson human beings and taking that process even further to
explore and articulate specific steps the Village can take to develop a safe supportive and identify
affirming community, in that regard the draft Village Statement of Inclusivity is a step in the right
direction and a starting point, but in her and others opinion it is not comprehensive enough or
does it offer the robust and meaningful support it needs to provide and strongly suggest that we
slow this important process down, and table the Statement of Inclusivity and that we agree to
continue to work collaboratively with our impacted community members, our experts and
stakeholders, our equity workers and our faith leaders to create a comprehensive program that
helps us to realize our aspirations for becoming a safe supportive and identity affirming Village.”

Mayor Pugh stated that in addition to residents coming to our Board meetings we have
also received correspondence which pointed out the material deficiencies and for that

BE IT FURTHER RESOLVED that the Village Board of Trustees makes the following
findings regarding the applicability of the LWRP policies and the consistency of the
Proposed Action with those policies and conditions of the LWRP which it found applicable:
POLICY 1: Restore, revitalize and redevelop deteriorated and underutilized waterfront
areas for commercial, industrial, cultural, recreational and other compatible uses.
POLICY 5: Encourage the location of development in areas where public services and
facilities essential to such development are adequate, except when such development has
special functional requirements or other characteristics which necessitate its location in
other coastal areas.
POLICY 5A: When feasible, development within the Village should be directed within the
current service area of existing water and sewer facilities or in close proximity to areas
where distribution lines currently exist.
POLICY 7E: Runoff from public and private parking lots and from storm sewer overflows
shall be effectively channeled so as to prevent oil, grease, and other contaminants from
polluting surface and ground water and impact the significant fish and wildlife habitats.
POLICY 11A: Erosion and sediment control measures shall be undertaken in order to
safeguard persons, protect property, prevent damage to the environment, and promote
the public welfare by guiding, regulating and controlling the design, construction, use and
maintenance of any development or other activity which disturbs or breaks the topsoil or
results in earth movement.
POLICY 25: Protect, restore or enhance natural and man-made resources which are not
identified as being of statewide significance, but which contribute to the overall scenic
quality of the coastal area.
POLICY 33: Best Management Practices will be used to ensure the control of stormwater
runoff and combined sewer overflows draining into coastal waters.
POLICY 33A: Encourage new developments to retain stormwater runoff on site so as to
not increase flows within the existing system or to improve existing stormwater runoff
systems to that runoff from such developments does not adversely impact coastal
waters.
POLICY 33B: Improve existing Village stormwater discharge to control flow of pollutants
from street and parking areas, etc. directly in the rivers.
